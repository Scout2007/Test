# Brief for the episode options, round 2

Round 1's options were rejected for three reasons, in the user's words:

1. "Each one feels like it's pushing the lore-exposing dialogue way too hard, and no one would sound like that."
2. "Each one feels like a rehash of the other variants, basically the same dialogue each time."
3. "They should all be doable with the 3D animation models from the main project; small additions and environments are fine."

This brief replaces the plan's old line that the series "teaches the film's rules". The series now **shows** things and never explains them.

## 1. Speech

- **People talk to each other, not to the audience.** Every line needs a reason for that speaker to say it to that listener at that moment, such as:
  - a procedure call, a question, an order or a complaint;
  - a joke or a sales line a real advert would use. Adverts sell feelings and benefits; they never list specifications.
- **Nobody states what the listener already knows.** Crews don't tell each other how their own gun works.
- **Numbers.** A number is spoken only when the speaker needs it for their own purposes: a range call, a countdown, a score, a time or a price. It is never spoken to inform the audience. Use at most three spoken numbers per option.
- **Budget.** Aim for 15–60 spoken words per option, and never more than 90. At least one option in each episode has under 15 words, or none. Count the words and state the count.
- **Picture and sound carry the idea:**
  - the wait is the time of flight;
  - nothing visible is the invisible beam;
  - a plume fraying is the field's reach;
  - glowing fins and someone sighing are the heat limit.
- **Subtext and character.** People are bored, tired, proud, nervous or funny. They talk about other things while the extraordinary thing happens. Understatement beats description.
- **Silence is a choice.** Vacuum is silent. The camera hears only through a hull it rides on, an instrument channel or a comm loop.
- **A test for every line:** would a real person in that job say this, in these words, here? If a line exists to tell the viewer a fact, cut it or turn it into behaviour.

Lines like these were rejected in round 1:
- *"Ten kilograms, twenty-five kilometres a second. It can't turn. A hundred kilometres is four seconds."*
- *"A corvette can't dodge inside eighty kilometres. A frigate, a hundred and ninety."*

Lines like these are the target:
- *"Range is hot."*
- *"…Anyone else hear that?"*
- *"Told you it'd dodge."*
- *"Logged. Go home."*

## 2. Three options, three different events

- **Separate events.** The three options in an episode are three separate events: what happens, where, who is there, and how it ends. They are not one event in three styles.
- **One idea each.** Each option picks one idea about the subject and shows that idea alone. The three ideas differ, and no option tries to cover everything.
- **No sharing.** No line, gag, beat, shot idea or speaker appears in more than one option.
- **Loglines first.** Write the three loglines before anything else. If one logline could describe another option, change it.
- **Seeds.** The seed premises in section 5 are your starting point. You may sharpen them freely. You may replace one if it can't meet this brief, but say why, and the replacement must stay as different from the others and must not take another episode's device (section 5).

## 3. Production: the film's 3D models only

- **Every picture is a Blender render of the film's 3D assets** (section 4), plus two kinds of extra:
  - **small additions:** props, a target plate or sled, a reflector satellite, a buoy, a loader arm, hull-mounted hit lights, signage, small set dressing;
  - **environments:** a test range among rocks, a station or dock, a planet backdrop, a hillside at night.
  Name each addition and say why it's small.
- **No new hero models.** No new ship classes and no rigged characters. The corvette has no picked design, so don't fix its look. People are voices only.
- **2D only as overlays:** titles, lower thirds, HUD, captions, a playback scrubber, simple graphics over 3D. No drawn animation, no cartoon, no stock or live action, no 2D-only shots.
- **Spoiler assets stay out**, even re-dressed: Breakwater, the Compact frigate and drones, Skerry, the Breakers, Anchor, the ring station, the planet Maren and the film's lost ships (Canterbury, Normandy). Generic rocks and other planets are fine.
- **Grades.** Old film, video, CCTV, macro or clay renders are all fine: a grade or a render override isn't a new asset.

## 6. Series rules that still hold

- **Look:**
  - beams are invisible;
  - lenses are violet only while firing;
  - blue appears only in the M-1C's bore pulse;
  - heat shows only on the fins' radiator faces;
  - in warp the fins are stowed and the shields on.
- **No spoilers:**
  - no Maren, Breakwater, Compact, Skerry, Breakers, Anchor, or anything from the dropped AI plot line;
  - no task-group names but the Astrid and the Endeavor;
  - systems appear in tests, drills, ceremonies and daily work, never in battle.
- **Tag (5 s, every episode):** a hard cut to the film's title card. OPERATION TIDEBREAK on the still stars, a mission-clock counter rolling from T−[n+1] to T−[n], the release date, and the card's low tone. Ep 9's counter reaches T−0, and the card's real second line takes over.
- **Runtime:** 40–90 s including the tag.
- **Names:** makers, presenters, yards and hull numbers stay [placeholders].
