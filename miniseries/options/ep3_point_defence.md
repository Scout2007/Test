# Ep 3 · Point defence (T−7)

Three separate events about one subject, each with its own idea. **A** is an old fleet training film that runs a six-round drill and strikes one dot off a chalk counter per layer; the idea is that no layer takes everything. **B** is a debrief in which a chief scrubs the replay of the one round that got through; the idea is that the gap was a person's. **C** is one missile's recorded seeker feed from lock to death; the idea is that it stays certain until the last frame. A has a sparse period narrator, B has three tired people, and C has no voice at all.

## Option A · *Six Layers*: vintage training film, 3D renders under film stock (65 s)

**Logline.** In a fleet training film, six practice rounds come at a ship and each countermeasure layer strikes one off the chalk counter, until the last layer has to do its job and the film jumps.

**The idea.** You leave counting dots. Every layer takes one and none takes all. The narrator knows the last layer should not be the plan, and says so dryly.

**Look and sound.**
- 1.37:1 pillarbox, black and white, heavy 16 mm grain, scratches and a hair in the gate. Locked tripod shots with slow zooms. Every shot is a held or moving 3D render. The leader and the intertitles are overlays on renders, so there are no 2D-only shots.
- Intertitles are hand-lettered type overlays (one line each). The six-ring diagram and the chalk-dot counter are simple line overlays. Nothing is drawn-animated beyond a ring lighting up.
- Mono optical sound with hiss and a library orchestra. Space shots carry only the orchestra. Layer VI is the one hull-camera shot and carries the CIWS whine.
- Narrator: warm, mid-century, a little tired of being right. He speaks four times.
- Look rules hold: beams are invisible, the lens is violet only while it fires (grain hides it), and heat shows only on the fin faces.

## Option B · *The Leaker*: debrief replay, tactical playback (55 s)

**Logline.** A chief makes a CIWS crew watch the one round that reached 8 km, over and over, until someone admits what happened at mount four.

**The idea.** A machine did not fail. A person waited for something to clear. The chief's temper is care, and the missile is a frozen speck while four people stay quiet.

**Look and sound.**
- The whole picture is the debrief screen: a 3D reconstruction in a flat-shaded override render with a playback overlay (scrubber, timecode, speed, DRILL REPLAY tag). The room is never seen, only heard.
- Replay audio is the hull microphone track: CIWS whine, with wow and flutter when scrubbed and a pitch drop in slow motion. The room is a ventilation hum, a chair and a cup set down once.
- Cool, flat grade; a green HUD with an amber warning only on mount four.
- Silence is a choice: the long beat after "Cycling." has only the hum and the slowed whine.

## Option C · *Seeker*: a missile's recorded feed (42 s)

**Logline.** A target missile's own seeker feed runs from lock to the instant it ends, with the ship growing in the frame and the tones going wrong one by one, and then the picture cuts.

**The idea.** A missile has no doubts. Each trick dents its certainty only for a moment, and it is surest in the last frame. Nobody says what happened.

**Look and sound.**
- A 4:3 window rendered at 640×480 as a seeker sensor, upscaled and graded in false colour. It sits in a pillarbox of telemetry columns (overlay). Brackets, a range counter at the missile's real closing speed, and a CONF readout.
- The ship appears as a dim silhouette with fin faces glowing. Heat shows only there, so the target is two or three amber panels in the dark.
- Sound is the seeker's own tones only: lock tone, stutter on jamming, a hunting warble, a flat carrier, a rising tone. No hull sound, no voice.
- No beam is drawn, and the missile's own body is never seen.

**Render.** The feed is rendered at 640×480 (about 0.15 of a 1080p frame): class D for shots 1–2 (≈0.15 h), class B for the flares (≈0.4 h), class A2 for the smoke (≈0.7 h), class C for the final fan (≈1.2 h), and class E for overlays (≈0.3 h). The total is **~3 h**, excluding the tag.

## Recommendation
I would pick **B**: it is the only option where the drama is a person's small mistake, the dialogue is the kind a tired crew would say, and it costs about 6 h. C is the cheapest alternate and carries no spoken words, which suits a set that needs a silent option. A is the safest period pastiche but spends the most render hours, and it overlaps C in the effects it shows. Decision for the user: whether the narrator's closing joke in A is worth about 5 extra render hours over C.
