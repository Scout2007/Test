## Option A: *Six Layers*, a vintage training film (75 s)

### 1. Concept
A re-issued LREF fleet training film, "revised edition: current fleet footage", in black and white with two hand-tinted inks, in which a warm narrator teaches new crews the six layers from the outside in. Point defence is a ladder learned by rote, so a patient numbered format lets the one rule land, and the grain hides first-version effects and the unpicked CIWS.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 1.37:1 pillarboxed in 16:9, gate weave, reel cue marks; the tag cuts hard to the film's 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Locked deck tripod and rostrum; crawl-zooms of ~3 % a second; 50–100 mm. The tripod justifies hull sound; the drone view is silent.
- **Grade and texture.** High-contrast black and white, crushed blacks, halation, heavy 16 mm grain, scratches, splice flashes. Two inks only: violet on a lens while it fires (hand-tinted), red for the rule.
- **Type and graphics.** Sign-painter capitals, white on black. Ink-on-acetate rostrum diagrams: flat shapes, numbered discs 1–6, a range ladder. No characters (not Ep 4's cartoon), no slates (not Ep 6's range film).
- **Sound and voice.** Mono optical track, hiss, flutter; a library march gaining an instrument per layer and dropping to one held note for the rule. [narrator]: warm baritone, unhurried, ~150 words. Only deck-tripod shots carry hull sound (servo ticks, capacitor whine, the CIWS whine).
- **Edit rhythm.** 5–9 s shots on slow dissolves; "Blind it. Fool it. Burn it." as a three-beat run; the rule gets the longest hold and quietest sound; the only true cut is to the tag.
- **Study.** US armed-forces training films, 1950s–60s (Prelinger Archives); Eames's *Powers of Ten*; British public-information films.

## Option B: *The Line*, a live sports-style broadcast (73 s)

### 1. Concept
A live, sports-grammar broadcast of a fleet point-defence trial: two commentators, a scorebug, a game clock counting down to the hull, a line-up card, replays, and a telestrator that must draw the beam nobody can see. Defence is a game of the last line, so the line-up teaches the six layers at a glance and a line review makes the 10 km rule the one call that matters.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9, title-safe; scorebug top left, game clock and layer scoreboard along the bottom.
- **Camera and lens.** Multi-cam: a 600 mm tracker as master; bolted ISO hull cams (50–100 mm, locked) that hear; a silent drone cam; a 2D replay plane. Whip-wipes, no handheld.
- **Grade and texture.** Clean broadcast HD, hard sun, deep black. Graphics in white, charcoal, signal yellow and broadcast red, never the world's violet, blue-white or orange.
- **Type and graphics.** Heavy condensed caps, tabular numerals, chalk-white telestrator dashes; the beam is a dotted overlay labelled BEAM (GRAPHIC); the 10 km line is a red goal line.
