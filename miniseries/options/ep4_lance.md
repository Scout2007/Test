## Option A: *Field Reach*, an animated technical-manual page (41 s)

### 1. Concept
One page of an LREF technical publication, drawn live by an unseen plotter: two figures, three numbered steps and a CAUTION box, with no voice and no music. A lance is a procedure with a hard limit, and a manual is the one medium that states a limit flatly.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** Full 16:9, the page itself: 12 mm margin rule, running head, footer "5-[00]", a change bar in the margin. The tag hard-cuts to the film's 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Orthographic only, locked. A "move" is a drafting move: a detail circle that enlarges, a page slide, an exploded part sliding on its dash-dot centreline. The one 3D shot is a front-to-side ortho turn of the bow.
- **Grade and texture.** Paper white, black hairlines at two weights, grey tint for section. Clay white for the model, Freestyle lines only. One accent, violet-white, used only for the field sheath (dashed) and the jet (solid). No grain, no red.
- **Type and graphics.** Condensed technical sans in capitals, in the plain "approved words, one fact a sentence" register of Simplified Technical English. Boxed FIG. numbers, index balls on leaders, dimension lines with ticks, a break mark where scale is skipped, a hazard-hatched CAUTION box. Each caption ≤ 12 words, held ≥ 3 s.
- **Sound.** No voice, no music. The medium's own sounds: a pen-plotter scrape that follows each line's draw speed, a relay click as each numbered item lands, a heavier clunk on a limit. The only tone is the field-status tone, a rising hull-borne whine while the sheath plots (the film's own "rising electric whine through the hull"). Nothing sounds in the diagram's vacuum.
- **Edit rhythm.** Step-locked: draw-on 1–1.5 s, hold 2.5–3 s, then a hard cut on the click. No dissolves.
- **Precedents (study, don't copy).** S1000D animated exploded views; Haynes workshop-manual figures; Apollo Operations Handbook diagrams; aircraft flight-manual caution boxes; Isotype pictograms.

### 4. Lore facts shown
Plasma needs an active field "lest it just fizzles out"; a lance has the ship generate the field, a torpedo carries its own [P1 §4]. Four nose lances [Model]; ~1,000 km/s, ~1 GJ, ~50 km [A-23]; the lance fires along the bow, so the ship must point [LREF §4.3]. It is a short-range, knife weapon [LREF K band, O12].

### 5. Assets and render
Reused: the Endeavor model for three ortho stills and the turn. New (series-only): a clay-and-Freestyle ortho set-up, a 2D manual kit. Estimate: 87 clay frames (84 turn plus 3 stills) × 15 s = 0.36 h, plus 984 composite frames × 2 s = 0.55 h: **~0.9 h** (the plan said 2.6 h; the plan-view turn is a 2D rotation).

### 6. Risks
- Plain by design: it holds a viewer only with the plotter's rhythm and the sound.
- A-23, a soft-SF reading, is stated as fact.
- Fig. 2 and Fig. 3 must stay boxes, or they become a new design that needs concept sheets [User].
- Four slots drawn from the front need checking against the built nose.

## Option B: *Design Review*, an engineer's pitch from blueprint to render (46 s)

### 1. Concept
The recording of a weapons design review: a calm engineer pitches lance integration over one drawing that matures from sketch to blueprint to wireframe to the film's own render, while two reviewers cut in and a red pen marks up the sheet. Plasma is finicky, and a review is where a limit becomes a requirement, so the "why only fifty?" argument is the whole episode.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** Full 16:9 "projected sheet" with an ISO-style title block (bottom right) and a growing action-item ledger down the right edge. The tag hard-cuts to 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Sheets are flat and locked, with slow 2D scans. One continuous 3D take at 85 mm: a slow dolly along the Endeavor's bow while a vertical maturity scan line sweeps across it, wire and blueprint left of the line, final render right of it.
- **Grade and texture.** Cyanotype blue with white lines for the blueprint; vellum cream for the sketches; red-pen marks; the render uses the film's hard sun on black. Violet-white only on the lance channels and the jet.
- **Type and graphics.** Drafting capitals for the sheet, quick hand lettering for the red pen, revision clouds, a stamp (PRELIMINARY, then APPROVED WITH A CONDITION), the ledger of RFA items.
- **Sound.** The room recording: air handling, a chair, a pen, a key. Three voices, no music: PRESENTER ([Lead Engineer]), dry and a little proud, on a close mic; REVIEWER 1 and the CHAIR from across the table, off-mic. The render plays with room tone only, because vacuum is silent.
- **Edit rhythm.** Hard cuts on the presenter's sentence ends; two interruptions overlap; one unbroken 7 s take for the sweep.
- **Precedents (study, don't copy).** Preliminary and critical design review packets; Grumman Lunar Module drawings; automotive clay-to-metal reveals; VFX breakdown reels that fade wire to final.

## Option C: *Keep It Together*, a mid-century educational cartoon (44 s)

### 1. Concept
A limited-animation public-education short from a [GUN public-education series]: a cheerful narrator and a jazz combo teach plasma as a gas that cannot keep its shape alone, with a bowling lane for a field. The lance is the lore's own joke (plasma is "finicky"), and a cartoon lets the physics be right while the tone isn't.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 1.85:1 inside 16:9 (a 1957-era theatrical short, distinct from Ep 3's 1.37:1 and Ep 5's 4:3); flat staging. The tag hard-cuts to 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** No lens. Flat cel stacks, panoramic pans, held truck-ins. Animated on threes (8 drawings a second), with smear frames for speed. The Endeavor is the real model with a flat, three-tone toon shader and offset key lines.
- **Grade and texture.** Gouache-flat fills, a plum-black ground with no stars, cream, charcoal, mustard and olive, and the violet-white accent for plasma and field. No blue and no orange glow. Paper grain, line misregistration, boiling outlines. No film scratches, so it doesn't borrow Ep 3's wear.
- **Type and graphics.** Chunky geometric hand-cut capitals, sunburst and boomerang shapes, pop-on label boards, a three-line recap card.
- **Sound.** A small combo (vibraphone, upright bass, brushed snare, muted trumpet) at 120 bpm, so shot lengths fall on 2- and 4-second bars. Narrator: a warm, brisk baritone, a friendly uncle, light puns, about 3 words a second. The drawn space is vacuum, so the band stops while the lance fires ("space is silent, so the band takes a break"), and returns on the next bar.
- **Edit rhythm.** Cuts on the bar, held poses, one smear frame for the jet, one strobe sequence.
- **Precedents (study, don't copy).** UPA shorts; Disney's *Our Friend the Atom* (1957); Bell Telephone Science Series; Saul Bass title design; Harold Edgerton's strobe photographs; Eames' *Powers of Ten*.

### 6. Risks
