## Option A: *The Multitool*, a late-night infomercial (90 s)

### 1. Concept
The builder's 3 a.m. infomercial for "one hull, three modules", with a loud pitchman and a co-host feeding him problems. It suits the subject because JCB's post is already a sales pitch ("just shove in the hot-swappable missile pod module!"), and the frigate's caveats (one config, 4 CIWS left, "does it really add up?") become the fine print that actually teaches the doctrine.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 4:3, rendered 960×720, inside a 16:9 pillarbox with a faint CRT bezel; the tag cuts hard to the film's 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Fast push-ins, whip pans, a dolly "product turntable" around the hull at 35 mm, before-and-after splits. The drone shots are a locked-off long lens (135 mm) so the pod ripple stays readable at SD.
- **Grade and texture.** Over-saturated studio video: floodlight magenta, cyan and gold against black, chroma bleed, interlace shimmer, tape wobble on cuts, soft bloom on specular edges. The hull's gold inlays (art-deco, per JCB) are the one true colour.
- **Type and graphics.** Chrome bevelled titles, starbursts, a red offer bar, a lower-third phone number ("CALL [number]"), star wipes, a tiny legal crawl. The crawl text is verbatim lore.
- **Sound.** A cheesy synth jingle (slap bass, brass stabs), studio-audience "oohs" and applause, a BUT WAIT! stinger, a cash-register ding on every "module". Narration: a loud pitchman (fast, warm, a bit hoarse) and a co-host (deadpan, feeding problems). Diegetic rule: the footage itself is silent; every sound is studio or voice-over, and the drone shots carry nothing but the jingle. The fine print is read at double speed, then slows for the key line.
- **Edit rhythm.** Under 3 s a shot for the pitch, then a deliberate drop to a slow 6 s hold for the fine print, so the joke turns sincere.
- **Study.** 1990s direct-response TV (Ginsu, Shake Weight, ShamWow, the OxiClean pitchman); QVC product turntables; infomercial parodies (*Tim and Eric Awesome Show*). Study the pacing, copy no brand or phrase.

## Option B: *Keel Day*, a shipyard commissioning broadcast (90 s)

### 1. Concept
The [yard]'s live broadcast of a frigate's commissioning: the hull glides from the dock, a module is hoisted into the bay, and a ceremonial voice reads the class's history. A ceremony is where a modular ship's "what it will become" is stated aloud, and it lets the film say the economic doctrine (frigates as the broad category, never the hero) in dignified words.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9 broadcast frame with a bug (the [yard] logo), a bottom third and a ticker; the tag cuts to 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Multi-camera outside-broadcast grammar: a locked wide (50 mm) from the yard gantry, a long-lens "crane" (135 mm) drift along the hull, a slow tracking shot on rails along the module bay. The shots are silent; a gantry camera is bolted to the dock structure, so it picks up clamp thumps and servo tones (structure-borne).
- **Grade and texture.** Clean HD broadcast, cool grey steel against warm sodium work lamps and white strobe markers, mild lens flare off the gold inlays. Slight compression blocking on fast moves.
- **Type and graphics.** Sans-serif news-event lower thirds, a serif ship-name plate ("[hull no.] · GARIBALDI-IVANOVA CLASS"), a module checklist that ticks off, a countdown to "BAY CLOSED".
- **Sound.** Brass-and-strings fanfare in the broadcast bed. Narration: a measured commentator, ceremony register, a half-second late on the pictures. Diegetic: the dock's PA and a yard-comms channel (gantry callouts, "clamps released") and structure-borne thumps; nothing in vacuum itself.
- **Edit rhythm.** Slow dissolves and holds of 6–8 s, building to a rapid checklist beat as the module locks, then a held wide.
- **Study.** Launches and naming ceremonies (the Queen Mary 2 and Artemis rollouts); NASA shuttle rollout and countdown commentary; shipyard livestreams.

## Option C: *Snap-Fit Fleet*, a scale-model-kit commercial (90 s)

### 1. Concept
A plastic model-kit ad from a [toy maker]: the frigate on its sprue, three snap-in module trays, a child-safe voice and a "collect the whole fleet" jingle. Modularity is exactly what a kit is, and the ad's rule that "modules swap, the hull is the one part you keep" plays the lore's thesis as a toy.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9, bright 1080p; a "toy aisle" desktop on a pale grid cutting mat; the tag cuts to 2.39:1.
- **Camera and lens.** Macro toy-photography: 100 mm, shallow depth of field, slow orbit around the model, a tilt-shift look on the desk. Digital-only "in-world" shots (the real hull on black) are the "box-art" shots, cut as a painted-style back plate.
- **Grade and texture.** Warm daylight, soft shadows, plastic subsurface, mould lines and tiny nubs where parts join. The model is a hull in grey plastic with gold decal inlays; the "real" hull on black is clean and sharp, cut in as the box art.
- **Type and graphics.** A boxy rounded display face, a starburst "NEW!", a sprue-number diagram, a part-count callout ("PART A1"), an age line ("AGES 12+; ADULT BUILDERS WELCOME"), and a "scale 1:1500" badge (~267 mm; derived from the ~400 m hull).
- **Sound.** Bouncy synth-pop with a chiptune lead; "click!" snaps as foley; a bright narrator (fast, friendly, trustworthy), a kid's "wow!". Diegetic rule: on the desk, everything is foley; on the box art in space, silence under the music.
- **Edit rhythm.** Bouncy 2–4 s shots on the beat, snap on each cut, a deliberate slow beat for the "one hull" truth, then a final spin.
- **Study.** Bandai Gunpla and Revell kit ads; LEGO and Airfix advertisements; collectibles ads with a "collect all" end beat. Avoid Ep 4's mid-century cartoon: no characters, no drawn animation.
