# Ep 6 · Nuclear warheads and their missiles (T−4)

Each option ends on the 5 s series tag (the film's title card, OPERATION TIDEBREAK in white monospace on the still star plate; the clock rolls T−5 → T−4 over [release date]; the card's low tone), inside the runtime. The platform end card follows (+5 s, outside the runtime): Ep 7 (T−3), the playlist, "Based on JCB's *Wearing Power Armor to a Magic School*. Ship designs by Sir_Lazz."

**Held throughout.** No beam is seen. No fireball and no blast wave in vacuum. Anything shot in vacuum is silent unless it is hull-borne or comes over an instrument channel. No people, no Maren, Breakwater, Skerry, battle or outcome, no AI plot line. Names are lore names, or a [bracketed placeholder]. Nothing is aimed at a planet's surface (ROE, doctrine §13). Ep 3 only draws a Casaba wedge, so this episode owns the Casaba.

**Assumptions shown on screen become canon:** A-24 (Casaba: 20–100 kt, 25 % into a 0.01 rad jet), A-25 (missile numbers), A-26 (~700 per hedgehog). Each option marks them.

**Lore facts shared by all three (what each shows, with sources)**
- Nuclear means "Casaba howitzers, conventional, etc", "much more commonplace and reliable than even plasma" [P1 §3–4]. Missiles are "a warhead delivery platform rather than a distinct weapon 'system'" [P1 §5].
- Bare burst: no blast wave; X-rays strip radiators and sensors to ~1 km (100 kt) or ~3 km (1 Mt); hulls breach only within ~30–110 m [LREF §4.4].
- Casaba: a 0.72 m hull belt breached from ~2 km (20 kt) to ~4 km (100 kt); anything in the jet's path is wrecked out to 50–130 km [LREF §4.4, A-24].
- Teller-device (thermonuclear) bare warheads go against radiator farms, sensor fields and drone swarms; Casabas against armoured targets [LREF §4.4].
- Capital-ship killer: 30 g, Δv 150 km/s, 100,000 km in 18 min, arriving ~110 km/s; a quarter of Δv held for jinking; hardened ablative nose, spin, three guidance paths (seeker, inertial, datalink via the destroyer net) [LREF §4.5, A-25]. Multi-pack: 50 g, 60 km/s.
- Decoys are powered, open a shroud to a killer's size and match its plume [LREF §6, A-27; MSL-V entry].
- Hedgehog: ~360 pods, ~240 killers and ~120 multi-packs of ~4, ~700 missiles plus MIRVs [A-26]; pods are ~6–7 m boxes, missiles no more than ~20 m [ASSET_REQUESTS MSL-V].

## Option A: *Standoff*, the contractor trade-show reel (55 s)

**Concept.** The loop a missile maker plays on its expo-stand video wall: glossy, confident, one word a beat. The hard-SF facts (no blast wave, standoff) are sold as features, which is how a brochure would present them and is why it suits the subject: the defence industry markets reach and geometry, never destruction.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 16:9 for a video wall, with all type inside a centre-safe 80 %. A thin cyan frame rule and a bottom ticker for the stand number and a looping progress bar.
- **Camera and lens.** CG orbits on a 85–135 mm virtual lens, smash zooms into callouts, speed ramps on flight shots. Hero shots are small subject on black with a cyan rim and one key; no stars behind a sunlit hull.
- **Grade and texture.** Navy and cyan, clean, with no grain and a slight bloom on type only. The missile is photoreal on black. SIMULATION sits in a corner of every engagement graphic.
- **Type and graphics.** A bold geometric sans in caps, kinetic: words slam in on the beat, numbers count up. Callouts are thin leader lines with node dots. Product mark: [PRODUCT NAME] by [CONTRACTOR] in a plain wordmark.
- **Sound.** Driving electronic score at ~120 bpm with a sub hit per word; it is built to loop, so the last bar resolves onto the first. Narration: none, except one processed single-word brand sting, "Standoff." The expo floor would drown a voiceover. Space shots carry no diegetic sound: the score is the medium's, not the vacuum's.
- **Edit rhythm.** Cut on the beat at 0.5–1 s for graphics, 3–5 s holds on hero shots. It speeds up through 20–45 s and drops to a long hold at the pod.
- **Precedents to study.** Defence-expo product loops, automotive launch reels, semiconductor keynote sizzle reels.

## Option B: *Range Report*, a declassified archival test-range film (55 s)

**Concept.** A reel of 16 mm and 35 mm range footage from a test series, released years later: slates, countdowns, high-speed replays and a dry range-safety voice. It suits the subject because the film's key facts (no fireball, no shockwave) are exactly what a range's instruments would record as an absence.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 4:3 Academy-ratio image pillarboxed in 16:9, with a visible gate edge, sprocket-hole flicker on the high-speed cuts and an overlaid frame counter.
- **Camera and lens.** Fixed tracking cameras on long lenses (300–600 mm), locked off, with slow pans following the missile. High-speed replays at 1,000–10,000 fps with a stated frame rate. Cameras are on a hull or stand, never handheld.
- **Grade and texture.** Black-and-white for range cameras, desaturated Kodachrome-like colour for the title sequence. Heavy grain, gate weave, scratches, burnt-in timecode, an occasional dropout where the film flares. The flash is a single overexposed frame, never a fireball.
- **Type and graphics.** Typewriter slate cards, a stamped DECLASSIFIED, range clock in white mono, a typed results block. Names are placeholders: TEST SERIES [DESIGNATION], [TEST RANGE].
- **Sound.** Sparse. A range-control voice counts down on the instrument channel (flat, clipped, radio-band). Telemetry tones and a Geiger-style detector tick are the only "space" sound, since both are instruments. A pressure-gauge channel is shown as a flat line, a deliberate silence at the burst. No score except a low sustained drone under the title. Narration: one dry archival male voice, minimal, reading the typed results.
- **Edit rhythm.** Slow: 4–7 s holds, one hard cut to white, then a slow 10,000 fps replay. The last 10 s is quiet.
- **Precedents to study.** Declassified 1950s–60s test-programme compilations, range-camera and film-leader conventions, laboratory high-speed film.

## Option C: *Exhibits 1 to 6*, a procurement-hearing exhibit reel (55 s)

**Concept.** A committee [COMMITTEE] reviews a missile buy, and we see only what is on the exhibit screen and hear the room: a chair, a member's questions and a programme officer's plain answers. It suits the subject because the questions an outsider would ask (why no big bang, why stand off, can't the defender shoot it down) are the ones the lore answers, so the film can teach by cross-examination.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 16:9, the room's AV feed: the exhibit fills the frame, with a slim lower-third exhibit ID and a clerk's timecode. No shot of the room or of anyone in it.
- **Camera and lens.** Exhibits are clean and locked: evidence-photo style CG on a mid-grey cyclorama with scale bars, line drawings, and one simulation marked SIMULATION. Slow push-ins of 2–3 % are permitted.
- **Grade and texture.** Flat, neutral, slightly cool. A faint projector keystone and fan-flicker on the screen edge, matte paper textures on drawn exhibits. Nothing glossy.
- **Type and graphics.** Serif exhibit stamps (EXHIBIT 3), red-pencil circle and arrow annotations, a typed index card at the end. Placeholders: [COMMITTEE], [PROGRAMME], witness: [PROGRAMME OFFICER].
- **Sound.** The testimony is the track: a close room mic with room tone, paper, a chair, a clerk's voice. Programme officer: level, patient, a little tired; chair: dry; one member: sceptical. Exhibits that show vacuum footage are silent under the testimony. There is no score. A gavel opens and closes.
- **Edit rhythm.** Dialogue-led: one exhibit per question, 4–7 s each, cut on the question's last word. The answer plays over the exhibit.
- **Precedents to study.** Legislative-committee hearings that played video exhibits, courtroom evidence presentations, and inquiry exhibit reels.

## Comparison and recommendation

| | A Standoff | B Range Report | C Exhibits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tone | Glossy, loud | Sombre, analogue | Dry, dialogue-led |
| Best at | Showing the missile | Making absence frightening | Teaching by questions |
| Render (raw) | ~3.5 h | ~3.5 h | ~2.8 h |
| Biggest risk | Celebrates the weapon | Overlaps Ep 1's test footage | Voices carry it |
| Overlap with others | Eps 2, 5 | Ep 1 | Ep 4, Ep 8 voice |

**Recommendation: C.** It is the only option where the lore's counter-intuitive answers (no blast wave, standoff, matched decoys) are asked out loud, so the film teaches itself and the viewer is ready for the film's missiles. It is the cheapest, and it breaks the series' run of glossy and vintage tones with a dry, voice-driven episode. A is the safe pick if the series must stay visual; B is the strongest mood but the closest to Ep 1.

**User decisions:** the style; whether the committee and witness may be generic [placeholders]; whether to show the ~700-per-hedgehog figure (A-26) on screen, which makes it canon.
