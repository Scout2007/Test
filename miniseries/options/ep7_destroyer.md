# Ep 7 · ECW destroyer (T−3)

Three separate events on the same unnamed ship ([hull no.]), each about one thing. *Quiet Hours* is a bridge watch hiding in a rock field while a search line passes: the idea is the hush as a thing a crew does with their bodies. *Who's Transmitting?* is a daytime fleet drill in which the destroyer's EW picture catches a friendly frigate's galley radio: the idea is that nothing leaks past the destroyer. *Shepherd* is the slow retrieval of the drones after a long sortie, ending on one that has to be talked home: the idea is patience and duration. The speakers, places, times of day and endings are all different. None of them uses the Endeavor, the Astrid or the Canterbury.

## Option A · *Quiet Hours*: hull-camera observation (50 s)

**Logline.** In a drill, a destroyer shuts down every emitter and sits in a rock field while an aggressor's search plume crawls past; the bridge whispers, and someone asks why.

**The idea.** The crew keep quiet with their whole bodies, long after a whisper could matter. Shown by the whispering and the long wait, never explained.

**Look and sound.**
- One locked camera on a dorsal mast of the destroyer, looking down the hull. It never cuts away. Slow 6 s push-in in the middle.
- Hard raking sun, deep shadow, rocks drifting slowly behind. The fins sit edge-on and cold: no heat on any radiator face.
- Sound is the internal loop picked up through the hull: air handling, a chair, breathing, whispers. Nothing from outside. Silence between lines is the point.
- Lines are whispered and close-miked. Only a lower-third caption (EXERCISE · GO DARK) and the mission clock are on screen.

## Option B · *Who's Transmitting?*: drill footage with comm subtitles (55 s)

**Logline.** In a fleet drill, the destroyer's EW picture lights up a friendly frigate: someone in its galley has the football on.

**The idea.** Nothing a ship leaks gets past the destroyer, not even a match. Shown as a ring growing on an overlay and a very dry officer.

**Look and sound.**
- Shot from the destroyer's truss, with the dish at the edge of frame. Clean daylight and a hard sun. The only movement is slow.
- The EW overlay (FX-EW) draws a thin ring that spreads from the frigate. It is a graphic over a 3D render.
- Hull-borne sound: a mug set down, a keyboard. The leaked broadcast comes through an instrument channel: tinny, murky, a crowd and a commentator you cannot quite make out.
- The destroyer answers by laser: a violet pin-blink from the truss, a violet pin-blink back. Both lines are shown in comm subtitles (SUB) with their callsigns.

| 2 | 8–17 | EW overlay: a ring blooms around the frigate and spreads toward us. Bearing ticks swing onto it. | Faint crowd noise comes up through the instrument channel. EW: "[callsign], your galley's got the match on." |

**Render.** Class A at 720p, about 14 s animated at 24 fps (~340 frames, ~1.9 h) plus overlay-heavy shots at class E (~0.2 h) and held plates: **~2.5 h**.

## Option C · *Shepherd*: a long day on the boom camera (70 s)

**Logline.** The destroyer's drones come home at the end of a long sortie, all but one on time, and the last is talked in by hand.

**The idea.** Looking after a fleet is mostly waiting. Shown by a clock running for hours, and a quiet voice for the one that is hard.

**Look and sound.**
- Camera on the end of a boom, looking back at the hull. The clock overlay runs the day (06:02 to 11:40).
- The sun sweeps slowly through the long shot (WORLD sun preset) and the shadows swing across the hull.
- Sound is only what the boom feels: thunks, servo whine, hum. Vacuum otherwise.
- The operator speaks softly and the bay hand answers once, tired.

**Storyboard.**

**Render.** Class A at 720p: time-lapse 12 s (~1.6 h), recovery montage 12 s (~1.6 h), final approach 28 s at 12 fps doubled (~1.9 h): **~5.1 h**. Cheapest cut: render the time-lapse at 12 fps (saves ~0.8 h).

## Recommendation

B. It is the most surprising and the funniest, and its overlay keeps the destroyer's one real trick on screen. A is the cheapest and the safest and is a good opener if the series wants quiet. C is the most expensive. If the budget is tight, drop C.

The user must decide whether the drill is allowed to be this light (a football match), and whether the DD rig really has per-fin posing for A.
