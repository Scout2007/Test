# Ep 7 · The ECW destroyer (T−3)

Each option runs 60 s and ends on the 5 s series tag (the film's title card: OPERATION TIDEBREAK in white monospace on the still star plate; the mission clock rolls T−4 → T−3 over [release date]; the card's low tone), inside the runtime. The platform end card follows outside it (Ep 8 (T−2), the playlist, "Based on JCB's *Wearing Power Armor to a Magic School*. Ship designs by Sir_Lazz."). All 60 s = 1,440 frames at 24 fps.

**Held throughout.** No beam seen; no violet lens; no heat colour (the destroyer has no radiators on show). Vacuum footage is silent unless hull-borne or on an instrument channel. No people (presenters are voices), no battle, no damage, no Maren, Breakwater, Skerry, no AI plot line. **The destroyer is never named**: the film has two, and the Canterbury is lost, so every option uses **[hull no.]** and never shows a ship going silent or dark. The dish is forward on its truss, as in Sir_Lazz's art (Q14 follows the art). Film check (storyboard revision 8): the film's destroyer shot (6) still carries only the line "Sensor net up."; no option quotes film dialogue, so revision 8's changes can't break anything here.

**Lore facts shared by all three (sources)**
- ~200 m, in a 100–225 m class; no main weapons, only CIWS and a few PD emplacements [P3; LREF §7]. Crews are loaned from GUN-EWCC [P3].
- The local network screen can silence "the radio-emissions of an entire fleet if present in large enough numbers" [P1 classes]; tens to hundreds of drones and satellites in its bays; three jobs: network and comms, sensor net, scrubbing fleet chatter and counter-EW [P3].
- The dish is "the eyes and ears" once the shield is off [P3]; the fleet is radio-silent and talks by laser mesh (A-29, D10, LREF §7).
- "The return to sender move" [P3]; the line "Wars fought through glass, and ended with math" [P3, JCB's own].
- **Assumptions on screen:** A-29 (EMCON plus a laser mesh; ECW enters only through a receiver) is shown as a diagram in every option and so becomes canon; D10 (two sensors, authenticated links, kill switches) is the doctrine's own.

## Option A · *Through Glass*: the crew safety briefing (60 s)

### 1. Concept
The briefing GUN-EWCC plays to every crew joining a task group, laid out as an airline safety card: four numbered rules and one calm voice, with the destroyer in a rounded inset. It suits the subject because EMCON is a rulebook before it is technology, and a briefing lets the series teach the film's rule (no radio, laser only) with a warm menace that the destroyer's "invisible" job deserves.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9, 1280×720. A safety-card layout: a pastel panel with a pictogram at left, numbered rule at right; the ship inset in a rounded-corner window with a thin white keyline. The tag cuts to the film's full-frame card.
- **Camera and lens.** Cards are locked and centred, with one-frame "page turns" (a card slides off left). The inset is a slow orbit at 85 mm around the stern quarter, never faster than 2°/s, so the dish and truss read. A held wide for the booms.
- **Grade and texture.** Flat vector pastels (powder blue, sand, mint, one warning amber) with a very light paper grain and fold-crease line down the middle. The inset keeps the film's grade, hard sun, black sky, so it looks like a window onto a real place.
- **Type and graphics.** A rounded sans (Nunito-like), a big numeral per rule, ISO-style pictograms (a crossed-out radio mast; a beam between two ships; a hand on a kill switch). Rule text is two lines at most.
- **Sound.** Soft lift-music loop with a two-note chime per rule. Narration: a calm, warm airline-safety announcer, a half-beat too slow, never raised. Diegetic rules: the inset is silent. Only the booms get a hull-borne creak, as if heard through the structure.
- **Edit rhythm.** Metronomic: one rule per 8 s, one cut per rule, no ramps. The only break in rhythm is the destroyer, held for 12 s.
- **Study.** Airline safety cards and the pre-flight video; the Metro Trains "Dumb Ways to Die" register; 1970s transit signage (the Isotype lineage). Study the cadence, copy no airline's wording.

## Option B · *Quiet Hours*: the listening post (60 s)

### 1. Concept
An instrument-room view of a fleet under EMCON, told with a spectrum waterfall, a waveform and a noise floor that stays almost empty. The destroyer appears only as its own noise: what the network screen is *for* is making the fleet sound like nothing, which is the one idea that can only be shown by what you cannot hear.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9 at 1920×1080 with 6 % title-safe margins; a single monitor surface with a thin bezel, no humans in the room. Panels: a waterfall spectrogram at centre, an oscilloscope strip below, a numeric readout at right. The tag cuts to the film's full-frame card.
- **Camera and lens.** Screen-capture feel: static, with slow digital zooms into a region of the waterfall, plus two camera shots of the real ship with an 800 mm long lens, locked and distant (the instrument's "optical channel").
- **Grade and texture.** Phosphor green-amber on near-black, scan lines, a faint sensor noise that the waterfall visibly carries as a dim speckle floor; the ship shots stay in the film's grade, with thin overlay tracking brackets.
- **Type and graphics.** Monospace labels (the film's HUD register), a frequency axis in [GHz] bands, a dB scale, a carrier tick that appears and is struck out. One conspicuous number at a time.
- **Sound.** The medium is audio-led. A sonified waterfall: a faint broadband hiss; a slow 40 dB-down tone "pad" under it; the occasional sharp spike (the "blip" the listener expects to find, and never keeps). Narration: a single flat operator voice, quiet, intimate, a log-reading register ("Log. Band three. Nothing."), always under 40 words in total. Diegetic rules: the ship shots are silent; sound exists only on the instrument channel and the hull contact mic (a low structural hum when booms move).
- **Edit rhythm.** Slow and hypnotic: 8 s holds that break once on a fast sweep, then a long final fade-to-near-nothing at the clock tag.
- **Study.** Submarine sonar rooms (*The Hunt for Red October*, *Das Boot* sonar sequences); radio-astronomy and numbers-station recordings; Ryoji Ikeda's data-driven installations; the Voyager waveform plates. Study the restraint, copy no title.

EMCON and laser mesh (A-29); the screen "silencing the radio-emissions of an entire fleet" [P1 classes]; scrubbing chatter [P3]; drones from the bay [P3]; "ECW can only enter through a receiver" (A-29, LREF §7); the motto [P3].

### 6. Risks

Never imply the ship's own noise gets lost (no foreshadowing).

## Option C · *Loaned from the Ranks*: the recruitment advertisement (60 s)

### 1. Concept
A GUN-EWCC recruitment spot with a spy-thriller tone: a low-lit voice, noir light on the destroyer, and the pitch that the most dangerous ship in the fleet has no guns. It suits the subject because the lore sells the destroyer as the humble ship that "can end a battle before anyone fires" and the crew as people "loaned directly from the ranks"; a recruiter's tease lets that confidence play with a wink.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 2.39:1 in a 1920×1080 container (1920×804 picture), like a cinema trailer; the tag opens to the film's full-frame card.
- **Camera and lens.** Slow lateral tracks past the hull at 50 mm, a long pull-back from the dish at 35 mm, a macro slide over a truss at 100 mm. One snap zoom onto the dish, the only quick move.
- **Grade and texture.** Teal shadows and a single warm sodium key on the ship; a lot of black; fine 35 mm grain and a faint vignette. Graphics are cold white on black.
- **Type and graphics.** A tall condensed sans (a Bebas-like), wide-tracked white, in the one-word cards ("LISTEN.", "SILENT.", "SEEN."). A thin redaction bar on the GUN-EWCC badge. The recruiting line is small and lower-centre.
- **Sound.** A low brass-and-synth pulse, a ticking clock sample, one sustained string note. Narration: a quiet recruiter, dry, smiling, almost bored, the voice of someone who already knows. Diegetic rules: ship shots silent; sound is score and voice only; the booms get a single hull-borne thud.
- **Edit rhythm.** Accelerating: 6 s opening, then 4 s, 3 s and 2 s cuts into a hard stop on a held black, then the motto at slow tempo.
- **Study.** Cold War spy-thriller title sequences (Saul Bass lineage, *Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy*, *The Bourne* trailers); modern military recruitment spots (study the structure, the question-then-answer pattern); the "you've been recruited" register of intelligence agencies. Copy no lines or logos.
