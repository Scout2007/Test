**Shared notes**
- **Names.** Only lore names appear: Astrid, Endeavor, Hanuman class (Ramayana-class heavy cruiser), LREF, GUN, EAF, JCB, Sir_Lazz. Everything else is a bracketed placeholder: [BROADCASTER], [HISTORIAN], [COMMENTATOR], [NAME], [RANK].
- **Film lines.** Storyboard revision 8 re-read. No film dialogue is quoted. The film's "Answer" (EWS profile, 135 mm, bow screen right, bloom at the bow) and "Spinal" (end-on, 200 mm, bloom down the barrel) framings are avoided. No option shows a hit, a target or a bloom down the barrel.
- **Numbers.** Lore: 1,600 m [P2], a spinal of "a good kilometer or so", 8 stacked fins, AVPSA "just over 100 metres", battlecruiser 2,500–3,000 m. Doctrine and working numbers (each shown on screen as an assumption, with its tag): about one shot a minute and ~2.3 TJ heat per shot (WN §4); ~30 s to slew 90° (LREF §10); 500 kg at 60 km/s (A-20); fins out at 1,200 K seen at ~15 AU by a 1 m cooled telescope (WN §5). - **Look rules.** Invisible beams; heat only on the fins' radiator faces; any bloom is blue-white and appears only in Option A, far and small. No warp footage. Vacuum is silent unless a camera rides a hull or a comms channel carries it.
- **Sir_Lazz.** No option puts the art on screen. All drawings are line renders of the built models (AST, EN).

## Option A · *The Pocket Battleship*: public-broadcaster documentary (68 s)

### 1. Concept
An older historian tells a [BROADCASTER] history segment on how the heavy cruiser went from glass cannon to pocket battleship, with drawings, one slow dolly along the Astrid, a thermal insert and one telephoto archive shot. It suits the subject because JCB's post is already a history of a ship class ("Also known as pocket battleships… glass cannons of old"), and a documentary is the form that takes drawings and dry understatement seriously.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9 master. Drawings sit in a 4:3 window on a warm grey mount; present-day footage is letterboxed 2.39:1; the thermal insert is a 1:1 inset with a thin white rule.
- **Camera and lens.** Slow dollies at 85 mm along the hull, never faster than the ship's size allows. Pans across drawings at 2–3 % of frame per second (a lock-off, then a creep, as if on a rostrum camera). One 800 mm telephoto archive shot, labelled compressed. Nothing handheld; no whip pans.
- **Grade and texture.** Warm filmic grade, lifted blacks, fine 35 mm grain. Drawings are cyanotype (white line on Prussian blue) and, for the old classes, sepia ink with paper tooth. Thermal is false colour (black to violet to orange to white) on the fins' radiator faces only.
- **Typography and graphics.** A transitional serif (a Caslon-style face) in small caps for lower thirds, italic for chapter cards ("I. The Glass Cannon"), thin rules, a dimension line on every drawing. Rubber stamps are condensed sans (NEVER WENT ANYWHERE). Assumptions carry a tag in the corner (WN §5).
- **Sound.** Solo piano with a low cello pad; the music is the only bed, dropping to silence for the flash and returning one beat late. Narration: [HISTORIAN], an older voice, measured, dry, slightly amused, no adjectives. Diegetic: none in vacuum; the dolly is silent apart from the music.
- **Edit rhythm.** Slow, 6–10 s shots, dissolves between drawings, hard cuts only into the thermal and the archive shot. The tag is the only hard cut in the piece that the music doesn't prepare.
- **Study.** BBC Four architecture and design histories; Ken Burns-style rostrum moves over still material (the pacing, not the sepia clichés); *Cosmos* chapter cards; the 2019 *Apollo 11* archive treatment.

## Option B · *Pass in Review*: fleet-review ceremonial broadcast (66 s)

### 1. Concept
A [BROADCASTER] live feed of a fleet review: ships pass the reviewing ship in order while a hushed [COMMENTATOR] and a formal protocol voice describe each, and the heavy cruiser comes last, taking an age to go by. It suits the subject because a review is peacetime pageantry, and the Hanuman class "finds particular popularity in peacetimes" [P1 classes]. A ship that is mostly gun, passing with its bore trained safely away, says the spinal cannon's story without firing it.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9 live broadcast at 1.78:1, a score bug top-left ([BROADCASTER] logo and a review clock REVIEW +00:42, not the mission clock), safe-area graphics, a thin gold border on bumpers.
- **Camera and lens.** Two fixed cameras on the reviewing ship's gallery (a 35 mm wide and a 200 mm tele), a locked pass with tiny operator corrections, a slow zoom on the heavy cruiser, one infrared camera picture-in-picture. A replay "slow-mo" is not used (24 fps film, no gimmicks).
- **Grade and texture.** Clean broadcast video: neutral white, slight highlight roll-off, pillowy bloom on running lights, interlace-free, very light compression noise. Navy and brass for graphics; the ships are warmly sunlit on black.
- **Typography and graphics.** A humanist sans (Frutiger-like) in white with brass rules; lower thirds slide in from the left; a ceremonial order-of-passage bar (ENDEAVOR · ASTRID) with the current ship lit; a bore-safe indicator (a green bar with a barrel glyph) in the corner.
- **Sound.** A military band (brass, snares, a slow march) as the bed; the music pauses only when the commentary does. Voices: [COMMENTATOR], low, warm, near-whispering, a state-occasion register with understatement; a protocol voice, clipped and formal, naming each ship as it enters ("Heavy cruiser Astrid, passing."). Diegetic: the reviewing ship's channel carries a single comms line, the Astrid's "Bore safe, trained off." Vacuum itself is silent.
- **Edit rhythm.** Long held passes (8–10 s) cut with two-second reaction cuts to graphics. The cadence follows the band's slow march, 70 bpm.
- **Study.** Royal and state broadcast commentary (BBC *Trooping the Colour* and Coronation coverage; US Navy fleet-week and international fleet review broadcasts); Olympic opening-ceremony order-of-parade graphics; study the hush, not the parody.

## Option C · *Pointing the Ship*: oral-history interview with archive footage (72 s)

### 1. Concept
A retired gunnery officer, voice only, remembers life on a Hanuman-class heavy cruiser as the job of waiting: pointing the whole ship, steadying, waiting for a target that cannot move. The picture is a black archive-catalogue frame and a few faded service reels, so the spinal shot is described and never shown. It suits the subject because the doctrine's deepest fact about the spinal cannon is its patience, and a person remembering a patient machine is more honest than a diagram.

### 2. Style bible
- **Frame.** 16:9 oral-history archive frame. Footage inside a 4:3 reel window (1.37:1) with a thin 16 mm gate edge and sprocket-free borders; the rest is black. The interview side is a black field with a warm lamp-glow gradient and a voice trace.
- **Camera and lens.** The reels are the ship's photographic unit footage from the service life: a locked 85 mm and a 300 mm tele, an occasional slow shake as if the camera sat on a hull. The interview frame never moves except the trace.
- **Grade and texture.** Reels: faded Eastman-style colour, magenta-tinted shadows, dust, a few frames of light leak, gate weave; the Astrid stays recognisable. The interview frame: warm tungsten, soft vignette, a faint hum-free silence. Drawings and graphics: a plain two-colour ink on cream.
- **Typography and graphics.** A monospaced archive catalogue line (ACC. [NO.] · TRANSCRIPT) in small caps at the top; the officer's words appear as burned-in subtitles in a rounded serif, word by word as spoken; a rolling reel counter. The lower third is a plain caption: [NAME], [RANK] (RET.), GUNNERY, HANUMAN CLASS.
- **Sound.** No score. Room tone, a chair, a breath, a tick of a kettle; the officer's voice, close-miked, an older man or woman, plain, laconic, a little dry, with pauses (the pauses are the performance). Where the reel is silent (vacuum), only room tone continues; a contact mic channel (a hull groan) plays under one reel, labelled. Diegetic rule: the reels have no vacuum sound; the officer's voice is the only soundtrack.
- **Edit rhythm.** Slow, with J-cuts: the voice starts before the picture. Three long reel shots of 5–8 s interleaved with 6–10 s of black interview frame. At the moment of firing the reel cuts to black with the voice finishing the sentence.
- **Study.** Documentary oral histories (Ken Burns's veterans' interviews, Studs Terkel-style audio archives); IWM and Library of Congress oral-history catalogue cards; Errol Morris's *Fog of War* for archive intercut (the intercut method, not the Interrotron).

### 4. Lore facts shown

### 5. Assets and render
Reused: AST with `fins_deploy`, `rcs_bow/stern`. Not used: FX-SPINAL, AVPSA tilt. New: the catalogue card and voice-trace graphic (2D), a reel-wear overlay (comp), and line renders.

### 6. Risks

Several lines teach film rules (standoff, time of flight) in an interview voice; keep them oblique to avoid reading as the film's plot. The officer is voice only, so the empty black frame must feel intentional rather than cheap. Ep 7 is audio-led, so Option C must lean on voice and archive, not on a listening-post layout.

## Comparison and recommendation

| | A Documentary | B Fleet review | C Oral history |
|---|---|---|---|
| Runtime | 68 s | 66 s | 72 s |
| Render | ~9.5 h | ~16 h (~13 h with crops) | ~8 h (~5 h shared reels) |
| Teaches | The class history and the "pocket battleship" arc | Scale and the turning, in pageantry | The wait: aim by turning, fire at what can't move |
| Spinal shown | Far flash, small, no target | None | None (reel runs out) |
| Strength | Clearest, closest to the plan and lore | Most visual spectacle | Strongest identity and mood |
| Weakness | Risk of lecture; the flash | Priciest; borrows ceremony | Depends on a voice performance |

**Recommendation: Option C.** It is the cheapest, it puts the spinal's real character (patience, and aiming by turning the whole ship) in a human voice, it keeps the film's climax image off screen so the film's shot lands fresh, and it fits the plan's "grave" place for Ep 8 in the run (T−2) better than a ceremony. If the user wants the plan's documentary, Option A is the safe, clear second, with the flash as the one decision to make. Option B is the showiest but the dearest and the one most likely to clash with Ep 5 and Ep 9.

**Open decisions for the user:**
1. Whether the user wants a flash on screen at all (Option A shot 7).
2. Sir_Lazz's art: permission still needed from JCB and the artist; none of these options use it.
3. Q15 (the Astrid's weapon count) stays unresolved; no option shows turrets.
4. Whether in-world inventions (bore safe, an order of passage, a retired officer's anecdotes) are acceptable as canon.
