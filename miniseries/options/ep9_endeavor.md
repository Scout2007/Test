# Ep 9 · Endeavor, the departure (T−1)

Three versions of the same night. **A** is the merge the user asked for, rebuilt: her failed messages home run the whole episode, while the station's departure loop plays through her ship and the task group pulls out behind the recorder, each ship given the same last words. It is the one option that shows the whole task group, so for this episode only it lifts the old rule that only the Astrid and the Endeavor may appear; it borrows C's message and B's loop on purpose. **B** is the working staff's alone: a tired traffic controller clears a queue of ships out, one by one. **C** is one crew member's alone: a goodbye she can't get through, over the ship's last hours. Nobody names the destination. All three use one new generic departure station (a straight lattice spine, not a ring), seen differently each time; A names it Rallypoint Delta.

## Option A · *Take Five*: a voice note and the departure loop (91 s)

**Logline.** A crew member tries five times to record a message to her mother, each take failing for a small reason, while the station's loop sends the task group out behind her with the same two last lines for every ship; she sends the plain fifth as her own ship pulls out.

**The idea.** A farewell is easy as a formula and hard as your own. Fourteen ships get the same two lines and go; she needs five takes for hers and sends the plainest. Nobody says where, and nobody explains the words.

**Look and sound.**
- **Frame.** 16:9 at 720p, night, one locked camera: her cabin port (a plain frame, a console edge below) looking along the departure lane, a line of buoys running out from the station's spine into the dark. Dim warm console light against a cold, hard exterior. The camera is on the Endeavor's hull and moves only when she does, in the last seconds. No people, no hands.
- **Recorder.** A 2D overlay over the whole piece: TAKE n, a running timer, a live waveform, DELETE and SEND. DELETE lights at each deletion and the take number steps; SEND lights once, at the end, and the strip reads SENT. The waveform draws whatever her phone hears, the loop included. Before take five it waits armed, the red dot dark.
- **Ships.** They leave in the order the film's arrival shot lists the classes, the Endeavor last. Near and mid: the Astrid, the three hedgehogs (Infinity, Pillar of Autumn, Galactica) and the two destroyers (Canterbury, Extenuating Circumstances; one hull, neither marked). Far silhouettes only: the Donnager, Excelsior and Nauvoo. The four corvettes (Rocinante, Tantive IV, Normandy, Wallfish) are pin lights and voices, never a hull. Fins stowed, shields on, no bloom: each ship eases onto the lane under a small drive glow, dwindles along the buoys and is gone. The Canterbury and Normandy get the same words, slot and weight as their neighbours, and nothing more.
- **The loop.** The station's departure loop is piped through the ship. Each ship's last exchange is CONTROL: "Stars above, [ship]." SHIP: "Always above, Delta." Five are in the clear, the Astrid first and the Endeavor last; the other nine, and one civilian freighter told to hold, sit faint under her takes, as through a bulkhead. A key moment is the word, laugh or silence where a take breaks: the loop is out for every one, a farewell begins only after one ends, and the Endeavor's exchange and her sent message are each in the clear. The station is named in full once, as a channel tag at the Astrid's call: RALLYPOINT DELTA · DEPARTURE.
- **Sound.** Her voice is close on the phone's mic with the hull's hum under everything; recorder tones are small (a falling tone on DELETE, a rising one on SEND). Vacuum is silent: the only exterior sound is each departing drive as a low thrum through the cradle clamps, lowest for the Astrid. The Endeavor's ring spins up as a rising whine, and the cut comes on its peak.

## Option B · *Cleared to Depart*: traffic-control loop (60 s)

**Logline.** From a dim control room, a controller clears five ships out of the station in turn, tiring as he goes, and gives the last one the only unscripted words of the night.

**The idea.** Routine. For the people doing it every day, a historic departure is a queue. The clearances shrink from a full sentence to a single word, and then the last ship gets a human line.

**Look and sound.**
- The one fixed shot is through the control-room window, with the console and a dim scope in the foreground. The scope is a 2D overlay of ring and blips; each blip vanishes when its ship goes.
- Exterior in silence. The room has the radio loop, key clicks and a console tone.
- Each ship leaves along a lane of lit buoys, at a walk, then blooms. Each bloom throws light across the window frame and console, a little bigger each time. The last is big.
- Grade: dim, warm console light against cold, hard exterior.
- Phraseology is real: callsign, "Departure", clearance, lane, readback.

## Option C · *Voice Note*: a message home (55 s)

**Logline.** A crew member tries three times to record a message to her mother while the ship around her is made ready, and sends the shortest take.

**The idea.** A goodbye said as small domestic things. We never learn where she is going. What she can't say shows in the two takes she deletes, and in the "Mum is typing…" that arrives too late for the picture.

**Look and sound.**
- The ship's last hours as three time-lapses, rendered on twos at about 12 fps, under floodlight on the station's night side. The camera is a slow, locked-off long lens.
- A voice-note app is a 2D overlay at the bottom: record dot, waveform, a timestamp. Nothing is on screen until she taps record.
- Sound: her room, heard close on the phone's mic, with the hull's hum under everything. The hum rises as the rings spin up and nothing else is heard.
- No bloom. The piece cuts to the tag before the ship goes.

## Recommendation

A: it keeps both halves running the whole way, her five tries and the task group's farewells, with nothing blooming, so the film's arrival flash is still the first the audience sees; the Endeavor, who arrives first in the film, leaves last here, and nothing on screen ties the two. B and C stay as the pure versions. You decide one thing: B still closes on "Good luck." and "Thanks, Departure.", which now contradicts the farewell formula; I left B as it was, as asked.
