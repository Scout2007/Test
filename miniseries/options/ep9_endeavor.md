# Ep 9 · The Ryland-class cruiser Endeavor (T−1, the finale, 75 s)

The task group leaves for somewhere the LREF won't name, and the segment hands over to the film. Every option ends on the 5 s series tag (hard cut to the film's own card *The record*: OPERATION TIDEBREAK in white monospace on the still star plate; the mission-clock counter rolls from T−1 to T−0 with the release date, under the card's low tone; it then gives way to COLLATED FROM THE AFTER ACTION REPORT, the card's real second line). The tag is inside the 75 s. The platform end card follows, outside the runtime: the playlist and "Based on JCB's *Wearing Power Armor to a Magic School*. Ship designs by Sir_Lazz."

**Held throughout.** No destination, no Maren, Breakwater, Skerry, battle or outcome. Nothing on screen is damaged, and no beam is seen. No Canterbury, no Normandy, no AI plot line. No people on screen, so every human is a voice. Vacuum is silent unless it comes through a hull or an instrument channel; music, narration and graphics belong to the medium. Only the Endeavor and the Astrid are named. Other ships are running lights. Anything not in the lore is a [bracketed placeholder]. The film's card text was re-checked against storyboard revision 8: "The record" still reads OPERATION TIDEBREAK / COLLATED FROM THE AFTER ACTION REPORT, and "Black, then a star" still opens on the flash with the L.R.E.F.S. **The departure look (open question 6).** The film defines only the arrival flash (A-04). All three options use one rule: the departure is the same FX-WARP bloom, shorter and cold, so the series' last picture rhymes with the film's first. It is never a streak or a tunnel. The shield stays on and the fins stay stowed; both warp rings are on the hull and brighten as `warp_charge` rises over 3–5 minutes (A-05).

**Lore facts shared by all three**
- The Endeavor is "a purpose-built exploratory variant of a typical Grace-class cruiser, known as the Ryland Class", with no spinal cannon; railguns, laser arrays, plasma lances and missiles [P1 §6]. The class is 950–1,400 m, "the workhorse of both the LREF and EAF" [P1 classes]. The model is 1,194 m [Model].
- Warp bubbles are made and held by two rings at the ends of the ship; a forward impact shield protects the hull "in warp" [P1 warp notes A–B; frigate post]. Radiators retract in warp [P1 §7; frigate post; LREF §8–9].
- The shield can be jettisoned before battle and collected afterwards; tenders carry spares [P1 §10]. Shown only as a fact about the plate, never as an event.
- Spool 3–5 minutes (A-05, assumed, shown on screen so it becomes canon). Hab ring spins at 2.4 rpm for 1 g [README `ring_rpm`; Model].

## Option A: *Undisclosed*, the live evening news (75 s)

**Concept.** A [NETWORK] evening bulletin cuts between an anchor, a live reporter on a station camera and archive clips, and is still live when the task group goes. It suits the subject because news is where a departure the service won't describe becomes a story about what we are not told, and it turns the film's first flash into the thing the camera was waiting for.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 16:9, broadcast-safe 90 %. Top-left [NETWORK] bug, red LIVE tag, station-camera label, FILE tag on clips, a two-line ticker, a lower third.
- **Camera.** Live feed: an operator's 300–500 mm lens, locked with small corrective drifts, a late focus hunt, never a perfect frame. The ship stays under a third of the frame. FILE clips are closer and steadier: 50–85 mm pushes, orbiting slowly.
- **Grade and texture.** Over-sharpened, compressed video. Exposure pumps when the ring light rises; blocky gradients in the stars; a faint interlace shimmer on the type only. No heat shimmer (no air). Archive is slightly softer and warmer than live.
- **Type and graphics.** Neutral condensed sans on a dark slate lower third with a thin accent bar; ticker in small caps; FILE and LIVE as chips. Time on screen is a ship-clock label; the segment's numbers are only A-05.
- **Sound.** A news theme sting at the open and a low bed under voices. Pictures are silent. The bed drops out at the whiteout, so the room "hears" the silence. A 1 kHz line-up tone on the signal drop, then nothing; the tag's low tone.
- **Voice.** An even anchor, unhurried; a reporter who is live, quiet and a little short of breath. Neither names the destination, because neither knows it.
- **Edit.** Hold-and-cut, 5–8 s per shot, clean cuts, no flashy transitions. The pace slows toward the whiteout.
- **Precedents (study, don't copy).** Cable live shuttle-launch coverage; election-night "live shot" cut-ins; archive "FILE" insert grammar; signal-loss slates.

## Option B: *Sent Home*, the crew's home-video compilation (75 s)

**Concept.** Phone clips and voice notes the crew send to their families in the hours before the jump, compiled for the families by the service, with one place where a message is censored. It suits the subject because the destination can't be told, so the pictures can be about everything near the camera, and the one thing the LREF will not allow becomes the gap in the letters.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 16:9 canvas holding 9:16 phone clips and messaging screens, with blurred-clip side fill; one split-screen of three clips. Rounded phone corners, a battery icon, a REC dot and timestamp.
- **Camera.** Hand-held phone: 26 mm equivalent, a rolling-shutter wobble, autofocus hunts, a window reflection of a dark room (no figure in it). Shots are taken from hab-ring windows and a weapons-deck port, through glass.
- **Grade and texture.** Small-sensor look: crushed blacks, lifted noise, purple-blue fringing around bright rings, auto-exposure that breathes, sharpening halos, macro-blocking in low light.
- **Type and graphics.** The phone's own UI: a system sans, chat bubbles, delivery ticks, waveform bars for voice notes, clip titles in a camera-roll style. The service's compiler adds a plain white caption and a black censor bar with [REDACTED].
- **Sound.** Voices recorded close, with the room (a low air-handler hiss, hull ticks) mixed under them. Hull-borne sound only: ticking and a rising hum through the window frame as the rings spool. No score, apart from a single held piano note on the final caption. A censor bleep is the medium's (the compiler's).
- **Voice.** Unnamed crew [CREW 1–5], casual, warm, a little nervous, speaking to a phone and not to a viewer. Nobody says where.
- **Edit.** Found-footage cuts at clip boundaries: starts mid-sentence, jump cuts, a dropped clip. 6–8 s, slower than A, with a held waveform in the middle.
- **Precedents.** Found-footage and "screenlife" films; troops' video-letter reels; space-station crew social clips. (Ep 8's oral-history is direct-to-camera and interview-led; these are off-camera and unprompted.)

## Option C: *Tonight*, the departure broadcast cut like a trailer (75 s)

**Concept.** [NETWORK]'s promo for its live departure special, cut like a film trailer: a held silence, a riser, title slams and a single drop-out when the ship goes. It suits the subject because it makes the departure an event of the kind a trailer sells (stakes, scale, an unanswered question), and it pushes the Endeavor closest to the camera of the three.

**Style bible**
- **Frame.** 2.39:1 letterbox inside a 16:9 broadcast frame, with the network's rating-style strip and a small [NETWORK] bug on cards only. Black title cards between picture beats.
- **Camera.** Glossy and deliberate: 85–135 mm close-ups with rim light, slow dollies, rack focus, one whip pan, speed ramps into hits. A single locked wide for the departure.
- **Grade and texture.** Cool teal shadows and a warm gold on the shield; deep blacks, film grain, bloom on hard highlights, shallow depth in the close-ups. Not the raw video of A and not the phone look of B.
- **Type and graphics.** Wide-tracked white capitals on black, one or two words a card. A tall serif for the title ENDEAVOR (in contrast with the series' monospace card); a thin counter clock in a corner, 21:00:00.
- **Sound.** A drone, a pulse and a riser, with one hit per cut; a single trailer-voice narrator. The score runs out at the flash, so the last seconds are silent, and the picture rests on that. Vacuum shots stay silent beneath the music's bed.
- **Voice.** Low, slow, spare, with no dramatic claims: it says only what is true and what isn't being said.
- **Edit.** Slow to fast to a drop: 4–6 s, then a 1 s montage, then 8 s of held wide, then silence.
- **Precedents.** Feature-film teasers (silence, riser, title slam); sport-event "tonight" promos; prestige-drama title sequences. (Ep 5's launch ceremony and Ep 8's fleet review are live ceremonies; this is a promo cut and never shows a ceremony.)

## Comparison and recommendation

| | A Undisclosed | B Sent Home | C Tonight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tone | Live, restrained | Warm, human, uneasy | Glossy, tense |
| Best at | Making the departure a story | Making the unsaid land | Hero shots of the Endeavor |
| Render (720p, raw) | ~7.5 h | ~4.5 h | ~7 h |
| Biggest risk | Least new | Voices only | Foreshadows dread |
| Hand-over | A live broadcast ends in the flash | A clip ends in the flash | A score ends in the flash |
| Overlap | None | Ep 7, 8 voices | Ep 5 gloss |

**Recommendation: A, refined.** It is the planned subject and the one the user liked, it is the only option whose live frame ends at the flash with no sleight of hand, and it keeps the finale's tone: restrained, with the series' grave episode before it and the film after. Its newness comes from the broadcast grammar (the ticker, the FILE tags, the signal drop), not the premise. Take B's one censored text line and graft it into A's ticker, and C's silent drop-out at the flash into A's shot 9, so the finale gets a human beat and the trailer's silence without a second look.

**Decisions for the user:** (1) the style; (2) whether the shield line may mention it comes off when the guns need the bow; (3) whether "fourteen ships" (the film's count) may be said; (4) the departure look: shorter, cold FX-WARP bloom, never a streak; (5) the tag's counter, T−1 to T−0.
