## Option A · *Delivered*: traffic-control loop and a voice note (88 s)

**Logline.** From a dim control room a tired controller clears the whole task group out in turn, the Endeavor last, while one of her crew records a message home in three takes and sends the third as her ship's turn comes.

**The idea.** To the people who work it, a departure the film will treat as an event is a queue of fourteen ships; to one of the crew it is a message she can't finish. The clearances wear down to clicks, and the only warm words of the night are a stranger's "Good luck." and her "Love you."; what she can't say shows in the two takes she deletes. Nobody says where, and "Mum is typing…" comes too late.

**Look and sound.**
- **Frame.** 16:9 at 720p, night. One locked shot through the control-room window, dim warm console light against a cold, hard exterior: the buoy lane, the queue beyond it thinning as ships go. A round scope on the console carries one named blip per ship, each going out with its ship. At the Endeavor's call, a hard cut to a longer lens, same spot.
- **Ships.** They go in the order the film's arrival shot lists them, the Endeavor last. Near and mid: the Astrid, three hedgehog frigates, two destroyers, the Endeavor; fins stowed, bow shields on, rings turning. Far silhouettes only: the Donnager, Excelsior, Nauvoo. The four corvettes are pin lights and key clicks, never a hull. The Canterbury and Normandy are called, answered and cleared like their neighbours: no extra shot, line, pause or place in the order.
- **Departure look: every ship blooms.** FX-WARP, the film's arrival flash, run backwards and silent: light gathers along the hull and swallows it, shrinks to a point and goes out. Far ships are pinpricks; each near one lights the window frame a little more than the last; the Endeavor's fills the glass. The film defines only the arrival (A-04); this makes the last picture before the card the flash the film's first picture answers.
- **Two sound layers, never talking together.** ROOM is the control room's own sound (the loop, key clicks, a console tone); the exterior and the blooms are silent. NOTE is her phone's mic, close, with the hull's hum. They trade turns: she records only in the loop's silences, a key click after each deletion tone opens the loop, and the Endeavor's closing click hands the floor back to her. Nothing sounds under "Good luck." or "Love you.": her recorder is off for the first; for the second the loop is closed and the hum has dropped out.
- **Speech.** Real procedure (callsign, "Departure", clearance, lane, readback), wearing down from a sentence to a name to a click, then back in full for the Endeavor. The note keeps C's three takes: a breath and a laugh she deletes, then the one she sends. No destination, mission or numbers.

## Option B · *Cleared to Depart*: traffic-control loop (60 s)

**Logline.** From a dim control room, a controller clears five ships out of the station in turn, tiring as he goes, and gives the last one the only unscripted words of the night.

**The idea.** Routine. For the people doing it every day, a historic departure is a queue. The clearances shrink from a full sentence to a single word, and then the last ship gets a human line.

**Look and sound.**
- The one fixed shot is through the control-room window, with the console and a dim scope in the foreground. The scope is a 2D overlay of ring and blips; each blip vanishes when its ship goes.
- Exterior in silence. The room has the radio loop, key clicks and a console tone.
- Each ship leaves along a lane of lit buoys, at a walk, then blooms. Each bloom throws light across the window frame and console, a little bigger each time. The last is big.
- Grade: dim, warm console light against cold, hard exterior.
- Phraseology is real: callsign, "Departure", clearance, lane, readback.

## Option C · *Voice Note*: a message home (55 s)

**Logline.** A crew member tries three times to record a message to her mother while the ship around her is made ready, and sends the shortest take.

**The idea.** A goodbye said as small domestic things. We never learn where she is going. What she can't say shows in the two takes she deletes, and in the "Mum is typing…" that arrives too late for the picture.

**Look and sound.**
- The ship's last hours as three time-lapses, rendered on twos at about 12 fps, under floodlight on the station's night side. The camera is a slow, locked-off long lens.
- A voice-note app is a 2D overlay at the bottom: record dot, waveform, a timestamp. Nothing is on screen until she taps record.
- Sound: her room, heard close on the phone's mic, with the hull's hum under everything. The hum rises as the rings spin up and nothing else is heard.
- No bloom. The piece cuts to the tag before the ship goes.

## Recommendation

A: it keeps C's note as the heart and B's queue as the picture, it is the only version that shows the film's whole task group, and it ends on the flash the film's first picture answers. B and C stay as the pure versions. You decide two things. First, whether every ship blooms as it leaves: I said yes (the film defines only the arrival flash, A-04); the alternative is C's cut to the tag on "Sent", with no bloom. Second, the order: the other thirteen leave in the film's arrival order, but the Endeavor leaves last although the film has her arrive first, and nothing on screen ties the two.
