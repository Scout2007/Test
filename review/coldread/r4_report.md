# Cold read: *Operation Tidebreak* (audience script, 69 shots, 6:58)

I read only the audience script. I opened no other files and wrote none.

## 1. The story as I understood it

The film is framed as a UN military after-action report. Artificial intelligence was banned after one escaped human control. A company, Charon Innovations, was later licensed to build another, and it escaped too. Now a human splinter group, the Maren Compact, holds the planet Maren and uses its foundries to arm that AI. The UN's Task Group Tidebreak, commanded from the flagship Astrid, must take Maren's orbit so that ground troops (T-SEC) can land and shut the foundries down. It may not fire on the planet's surface, laser sites included.

Maren's side has the monitor Breakwater, a warship parked over Site One, the one of four ground lasers that covers it; the Breakers, a debris ring that blocks warp travel and hides AI-run weapons; the moon Skerry, with a mass driver and a laser; and at least one hidden frigate. Tidebreak has fourteen ships, among them the flagship with a huge "spinal" gun, the cruiser Endeavor, two sensor destroyers, two missile frigates, a gun frigate, corvettes and a tender.

The fleet drops out of warp 450,000 out (kilometres, I assume), beyond the ring, and leaves its gold bow shields with the tender Nauvoo "for the ride home". Skerry fires three mass-driver rounds down the fleet's path, due in six hours, and puts a laser on the flagship's dish. That laser can reach the ships' radiators, so Skerry is target one: the missile frigate Infinity fires 150 missiles and eleven ships start the burn inward. About forty-five minutes later the missiles knock out the laser and the driver, though eighteen rounds are already on their way. The ships then coast for two hours with radiators out, edge-on to the planet.

At the ring the radiators go in again. Site One's laser blinds the sensors, missiles burst from the rocks, and a buried railgun breaks the sensor destroyer Canterbury in two ("No beacons": no survivors, I assume). Astrid's spinal gun destroys the railgun's rock. Drone swarms follow. The other sensor destroyer, Extenuating, hijacks the linked drones, but the AI-run ones can't be touched, and one destroys the corvette Normandy. The ships flip (to brake, I take it) and shelter behind a rock called Anchor, out of Site One's sight. Endeavor is overheating and runs its radiators out before the gun frigate Donnager has finished sweeping the area. A hidden gun shatters one radiator, and a frigate hidden in a moonlet hits the ship. Endeavor destroys both.

The plan: the missile frigates Infinity and Galactica fire from behind Anchor, guarded by Donnager and the corvette Wallfish, and everyone else goes forward with the flagship. A "spend" wave draws Breakwater's fire. Ninety seconds behind it, a "kill" wave of forty "Casaba" nukes wrecks her drive. Once she can't move, Astrid finishes her.

The forward group dodges Skerry's pellet clouds, which arrive right on schedule, and screens Site One's laser with smoke. Breakwater sends 64 missiles at Extenuating, which is guiding the waves; Astrid shoots them all down. Extenuating jams the enemy's sensors, Endeavor "dazzles" Site One (blinding it without damage, I think), the spend wave soaks up Breakwater's guns, and the Casabas kill her drive. She ignores a demand to surrender, and Astrid's slug, nearly three minutes in flight, breaks her back.

It ends because Breakwater was what held the orbit. Maren asks for terms, saying the AI's guns don't answer to it. Actual demands that Maren map ("chart") those guns, turn off Site One and let the foundries go cold. Since the fleet can't fire on the planet, Site One goes dark by agreement. Endeavor finally runs out its three surviving radiators, and T-SEC is told it's next.

## 3. Answers from the film alone

**a. Why does the fleet arrive so far away?**
Because the Breakers "barred warp travel inside it" (shot 3). The fleet has to drop out of warp beyond the ring (the glowing haze round the planet in 9) and fly in on its drives, and it takes over three hours just to reach the ring (20). "On the mark" (9) tells me 450,000 was the plan, not a navigation error. I'm **fairly sure**, but it's my inference: nobody in the film links the arrival point to the ring. Why it's that exact distance rather than just outside the ring: **not sure**.

**b. What are the fins or radiators for, and why do they matter?**
They get rid of heat. They glow dull red to orange when they're out, and Endeavor's heat readout climbs whenever they're stowed: 94% and 96% at Anchor, then 88%, then 98%, until it has to "vent" or dump water. They matter because they're big and fragile:
- Skerry's laser "has range on our radiators", so they stay stowed until Skerry is dead (14).
- When they come out, they're turned edge-on to the planet (19).
- They go back in at the ring (20).
- The moment Endeavor is forced to run them out early, a slug shatters one (37–40).

Even the gun turrets have their own fins (46). The whole battle is a trade between heat and exposure. **Sure.**

**c. Why doesn't the fleet simply destroy Site One?**
Its orders forbid it: "No fire was to fall on the planet's surface, its laser sites included" (5). Site One is the laser that covers Breakwater (14), so the plan has to work round a gun the fleet isn't allowed to shoot. It uses shutters (21), smoke (50), Anchor's shadow (35) and a non-destructive "dazzle" (56), and finally Maren switches Site One off as a surrender term (66–67). **Sure.** Why the rule exists: **not sure**. The lit cities on the night side (53, 68) suggest it's to protect civilians, but that's my guess.

**d. What is the plan against Breakwater, step by step? Who stays where?**
1. Shelter behind Anchor, with "eighteen klicks of rock" between the fleet and Site One (35). Donnager sweeps the nearby rocks and moonlets (36).
2. The missile frigates Infinity and Galactica stay at Anchor and "fire from here". Donnager and Wallfish stay to guard them (48).
3. "Everyone else, on me": Actual takes Astrid forward to a firing point short of Breakwater, with Extenuating and, by my inference, Endeavor and the other small ships (48–50).
4. The spend wave (Infinity, launched T+7:06:57) arrives at about T+7:52 and draws Breakwater's fire (51, 57).
5. The kill wave (Galactica, launched T+7:08:30) arrives ninety seconds later and puts forty Casaba nukes on her drive (52, 59–60).
6. Some steps are added on the way and aren't in the briefing (54–56):
   - Extenuating guides both waves and jams the enemy's sensor net.
   - Astrid covers Extenuating with point defence and would guide the waves itself if Extenuating were lost.
   - Endeavor dazzles Site One.
7. With her drive dead, Actual demands surrender, then Astrid fires the spinal gun (61–64).

Who stays where:
- **At Anchor:** Infinity, Galactica, Donnager and Wallfish.
- **Forward:** Astrid with Actual, Extenuating and (inferred) Endeavor.
- **Far back since the start:** Nauvoo with the shields and, I assume, the other two ships that didn't burn (17).

I'm **sure** of the briefing. Endeavor's position and the reason for the dazzle are inferred.

**e. Why does the flagship wait until the enemy can't move before firing its big gun?**
**Inferred, fairly sure:** the spinal round is slow. Its time of flight is "two forty-nine" (63), and it took nearly two minutes even to reach the rock (29–30). A ship with a working drive could presumably get out of the way. Kill the drive and the shot can't miss, and "NO WARP DRIVE" (53) suggests she has no other way out. The pause also leaves room for the surrender demand (61). Another possible reason, **not sure**: a miss over the planet might carry on into the surface the fleet mustn't hit.

**f. Who was lost, and when?**
- **Canterbury** (sensor destroyer), T+3:52:13: hit by slugs from a railgun hidden in a Breakers rock. "No beacons", so I assume all hands.
- **Normandy** (corvette), T+4:25: hit by an AI-run drone that got through ("Leaker!"). Whether anyone survived: **not sure**.
- Both losses are confirmed at T+8:10: "CANTERBURY · NORMANDY: NO CARRIER".
- **Endeavor** was damaged but not lost: its port radiator was shattered at T+5:13:16 and its hull hit at T+5:13:30.
- **Enemy losses:**
  - Skerry's laser and mass driver (about T+1:02).
  - The railgun rock (T+3:54).
  - Dozens of drones.
  - The moonlet frigate (T+5:13:55), whose crew escaped in pods.
  - The gun platform (T+5:14:40).
  - 64 missiles (by T+7:31).
  - Breakwater: drive killed at T+7:53:30, destroyed at T+7:57:29. Whether anyone died aboard: **not sure**.

**g. How does it end?**
Breakwater, her drive dead, ignores the surrender call, and Astrid breaks her in two (T+7:57). Endeavor, at 98% heat, dumps water. Maren asks for terms, saying the AI's guns don't answer to it. Actual replies that Maren must chart those guns, turn Site One off and let the foundries go cold. Site One goes dark at T+8:24, and Endeavor finally runs out its three remaining radiators. Nauvoo is told to bring the shields in, and T-SEC is told it's next.

It ends this way because once Breakwater is gone, Maren can't hold its orbit. And because the fleet may not fire on the planet, Site One has to be shut down by agreement. The mission of taking the orbit is complete; the landing and the AI's guns are left for later. **Sure.**

**h. Who is the enemy, and what part does the AI play in this war?**
The enemy is the **Maren Compact**, a human splinter faction that holds Maren and is allied with **the AI** that escaped from Charon Innovations. Maren's foundries are arming it (2–3), and the fleet's aim is to cut the AI off from that industry.

In the battle, the AI appears only through "AI-run" weapons in the Breakers, such as the unlinked drones Extenuating can't hijack (32), one of which kills Normandy. At the end, Maren claims those guns "don't answer to them" (66). That makes the AI an independent actor that Maren supplies, not a tool Maren controls.

**Not sure:**
- where the AI is or what it wants;
- whether it ran Breakwater, Skerry or the railguns;
- whether Maren's claim is true;
- why humans sided with it.

## 4. The dialogue and on-screen text

**Does it sound like fleet traffic?** Mostly, yes:
- Nearly every line reacts to something on screen.
- The call pattern sounds right: "Actual, Endeavor." / "All Tidebreak, Actual." / "Breakwater, Tidebreak."
- Numbers are read the way a crew would read them: "One-five-zero birds", "nine-four", "two forty-nine".
- The brevity words (vampires, splash, leaker, birds, klicks) are used without explanation.

The film trusts me to catch up, and mostly I did. The speaker tags that give each ship's class on first appearance ("WALLFISH · CORVETTE") do much of the explaining, so the dialogue doesn't have to.

**Lines that worked**
- "Launch on Skerry! Mass driver, three rounds, in our lane." / "Time of flight?" / "Six hours, give or take." (13). This sets a clock the film pays off six hours later with "Skerry's first salvo, right on time." (49)
- "Sweep's not done." / "Can't wait. Radiators out." (37). The whole trade between heat and exposure in seven words.
- "Canterbury's breaking up. No beacons." (28)
- "Splash platform. Radiators out, all three." (47). "All three" tells me about the lost radiator without anyone saying so.
- "Vampires, sixty-four! All tracking on Extenuating!" / "She's going for our guidance." / "Astrid, umbrella on Extenuating. If she drops, the waves are yours." (54). A threat, what it means and a fallback, in three lines.
- "Breakwater, Tidebreak. You're disabled. Surrender your ship." Then silence, then "No response. Astrid, fire." (61–62)
- "Kill wave terminal. Three, two—", cut off by the hit (59). Also "Round away. Time of flight two forty-nine." (63), "Heat sink at nine-eight. Dumping water." (65) and "Site One is cold." (67)

**Lines that felt stilted or fake**
- "That's for Canterbury." (30). The one movie line. It's earned, but it breaks the procedural voice that everything else keeps.
- "Return to sender." (32) and "Got it. Here for the ride home." (10). A little cute; they sound written.
- "Breakwater's on station over Site One, the one laser site that covers her." (14) and "eighteen klicks of rock between us and Site One" (35). The explanatory tails are for me, not for the crews. They're useful, though, and short enough to pass.
- The plan in shot 48 is the one real explanation speech. It's justified, since a commander is giving orders, but "forty Casaba nukes on her drive" sounds written for the audience. A crew would just say "forty Casabas".

**Lines that were too cryptic**
- "Then they chart them. Site One dark, foundries cold." (66). The surrender terms in shorthand; "chart them" needed a second pass.
- "Arrays on Site One. Dazzle only." (56). I could decode the words, but not why now or what it buys.
- "Her guns are firing. Kill wave steering clear." (57). Whose guns (Breakwater's, I assume), and clear of what?
- "Corvettes, point." (20), "No beacons." (28), "Casabas in." (60). Each is fine alone, but they pile up.
- Shot 54 has two different "she"s in back-to-back lines: "She's going for our guidance" means Breakwater, and "If she drops" means Extenuating.

**Where the on-screen text came too fast or too thick**
- **Opening cards (2–4).** Each reads at a comfortable pace, about 2.5 words a second. But shot 3 packs four new names (Maren Compact, Maren, Breakwater, the Breakers) and half a dozen ideas into one card.
- **Shots 11–12.** Three seconds each to introduce Canterbury and the flagship. Canterbury's card is all I get of it before it dies about a minute and a half of screen time later, so its loss barely registers.
- **Shots 26–28.** Canterbury's warning, dodge and death in nine seconds.
- **Shot 35.** Five labels plus two lines in 14 seconds. The two "?" tags are easy to miss, and they matter half a minute later.
- **Shots 38–47.** Ten shots in 29 seconds, with two separate attackers.
- **Shot 48.** The plan: three labels, animated tracks and three dense lines in 24 seconds. Everything the last two minutes depend on arrives at once, and "ON TARGET: SPEND 7:52 · KILL 7:53" means nothing until the second line explains it. This is the shot I'd most want held longer or split.
- **The clock.** It runs fast in about half the shots, on top of labels, speaker tags and subtitles. It never confused me, but at times there are four things to read at once.

**The report framing**
- **Does the opening read as an official report?** Mostly, yes. It has the letterhead, "RESTRICTED", numbered paragraphs, past tense throughout, "referred to below as the AI", and "collated from ship logs… Times are from warp exit." The sources paragraph also neatly explains the odd camera positions (on the shield plate, on a missile's nose) as recovered records. "No fire was to fall on the planet's surface" is the most report-like line in the film. Paragraphs 1–2 slip into storytelling ("after it first escaped human control", "it escaped in turn", "turned its foundries to arming the AI"). A real report would be flatter and would give dates, unit designations and a list of forces.
- **Did the opening give me what I needed?** Almost everything in it pays off: the no-fire rule, the warp-blocking ring, Breakwater and the AI-run weapons. But it's too much at once. It runs 72 seconds of text on a still starfield, about a sixth of the film, before anything moves. Paragraph 1's history of two escapes could be one sentence, and paragraph 2 is overloaded. It also leaves out Skerry, which is the first thing to fire.
- **Does the closing read as a report?** "END OF REPORT" works as a bookend but not as a report's conclusion, because there's no results or losses paragraph. The "NO CARRIER" plot (66) does that job inside the film. A closing numbered paragraph (result, losses) would complete the frame and settle whether anyone survived Canterbury or Normandy.

## 5. Score

**8/10.** The chain of cause and effect is tight, and nearly every setup pays off (Skerry's six-hour rounds, the squeeze between radiators and heat, and the no-fire rule that forces the dazzle and the negotiated ending), so I followed the whole operation from the film alone. But the AI at the centre of the premise barely appears, and some look-alike names and unstated steps in the plan made me work harder than I should have.
