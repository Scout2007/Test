# Operation Tidebreak: first-viewing report

This is based only on the audience script (69 shots, 6:40). Where I'm inferring something, or relying on what I know of the genre, I say so.

## 1. The story as I understood it

**Who and why.** The film is framed as a United Nations after-action report. AI was outlawed after it first escaped human control. A later, licensed system from Charon Innovations escaped too, and the report calls it "the AI". On the planet Maren, a breakaway human faction called the Maren Compact has sided with the AI and arms it from Maren's foundries. UN Task Group Tidebreak must take Maren's orbit so ground troops (T-SEC) can land and shut the foundries down. The catch is that nothing may be fired at the planet's surface, so its ground lasers can only be dazzled, never destroyed.

**What each side has.** The UN brings fourteen ships:
- the flagship Astrid, which carries the commander ("Actual") and a kilometre-long gun;
- the cruiser Endeavor, with lasers, gun turrets and a plasma "lance";
- two "E-war" destroyers, Canterbury and Extenuating;
- the missile frigates Infinity and (I infer) Galactica;
- the gun frigate Donnager;
- corvettes, including Wallfish and Normandy;
- a tender, Nauvoo, which looks after the ships' detachable gold bow "shields".

The ships get rid of heat through fragile radiator fins.

Maren has:
- the Breakers, a debris ring that blocks warp travel and hides weapons in its rocks;
- a moon, Skerry, with a mass driver and a laser;
- a ground laser, Site One;
- at least one frigate (crewed, since it has escape pods);
- Breakwater, a big Compact warship with no warp drive, parked in orbit under Site One's protection.

**What happens.** The fleet drops out of warp 450,000 out (kilometres, I assume) and leaves its shields with Nauvoo. Within minutes, Skerry fires mass-driver rounds into the fleet's path, due in six and a half hours, and hits Astrid's dish with its laser. Infinity sends 150 missiles at the moon as the fleet burns in. Within the hour, Skerry's laser and gun are dead, but eighteen rounds are still coming.

The ships coast with their radiators out, then pull them in at the Breakers. There, Site One blinds Endeavor's sensors, missiles burst from the rocks, and a hidden railgun breaks Canterbury in half, apparently with no survivors. Astrid's big gun destroys the railgun's rock. Drones pour out. Extenuating takes over the remotely linked ones, but the AI-run ones can't be touched, and one gets through and kills the corvette Normandy.

The fleet flips (to brake, I assume) and hides behind a rock called Anchor, out of Site One's sight. Endeavor is overheating and puts out its radiators before the area is cleared. A hidden gun shoots one off and a frigate ambushes it. Endeavor destroys both.

The plan against Breakwater is simple. The missile frigates stay at Anchor under guard while the rest advance. A first missile wave soaks up Breakwater's defences, a second puts forty nuclear warheads on her engines, and Astrid finishes her. Skerry's rounds are dodged or miss. Breakwater fires sixty-four missiles at Extenuating, which is guiding the waves, and Astrid shoots them all down. The kill wave cripples Breakwater. She doesn't answer a call to surrender, and Astrid's round breaks her apart almost three minutes later, just as she comes into sunlight.

**How it ends.** Maren asks for terms and says the AI's guns don't take its orders. The commander demands that those guns be mapped, Site One switched off and the foundries shut. Site One goes dark. Endeavor, at 98% heat, dumps water, then puts out its three remaining radiators. The shields are called back in and T-SEC is told it's next. My inference on why it ends there: with Breakwater gone, Maren has nothing left to keep the fleet out of its orbit.

## 3. Questions answered from the film alone

**a. Why does the fleet arrive so far away?** The SITUATION card (shot 3) says the Breakers block warp travel inside the ring. So the fleet has to leave warp outside it and fly in on its drives, and "on the mark" says the arrival point was planned. Why the mark is as far out as 450,000 (km, I assume), rather than just outside the ring: not sure.

**b. What are the fins or radiators for, and why do they matter?** They get rid of the ship's heat. They glow when they're out, and Endeavor's heat drops from 96% to 31% once its three are out (37–48). They matter because they're the weak point:
- lasers can burn them (14), and a slug shatters one (40);
- so ships pull them in under fire (20, 39), and the heat climbs (94%, then 96%, then 98%);
- that forces Endeavor into risky choices: putting them out before the sweep is done (37), and later dumping water (65);
- Endeavor ends the film with three fins instead of four (68).

The guns put out their own fins after firing (46), which I take to mean that firing itself makes heat (inference). What happens at 100%: not sure.

**c. Why doesn't the fleet simply destroy Site One?** Because the rules (the RESTRICTIONS card, shot 5) forbid any fire on the planet's surface, "laser sites included; they could be dazzled, not struck". So the fleet shutters its sensors, lays smoke, hides behind Anchor and dazzles Site One (56). In the end Maren switches it off (66–67). Why the rule exists: not sure. My guess is the cities visible on the night side (53, 68).

**d. The plan against Breakwater, step by step:**
1. The missile frigates (Infinity, Galactica) stay in Anchor's shadow, guarded by Donnager and Wallfish.
2. Everyone else goes with Actual, heading for Astrid's firing point short of Breakwater. That's Astrid, Endeavor, Extenuating and presumably any remaining corvette.
3. Infinity fires the spend wave at T+7:06:57, due at 7:52:00, to draw Breakwater's defensive fire.
4. Galactica fires the kill wave at T+7:08:30, due at 7:53:30: forty Casaba nukes aimed at her drive.
5. This part isn't in the briefing but is shown later (54–56). Extenuating guides the waves. Astrid covers Extenuating ("umbrella") and is apparently the backup for guiding the waves. Endeavor's lasers go full power on Breakwater and only dazzle Site One.
6. Once Breakwater's drive is dead, Astrid fires its big gun.

Nauvoo waits far out with the shields. I assume so do the two other ships that stayed dark in shot 17.

**e. Why wait until the enemy can't move?** This is my inference, and I'm fairly confident of it. Astrid's round is slow: about two minutes to the railgun rock (29–30) and 2 min 49 s to Breakwater (63). A ship with a working drive could simply move out of the way, as Canterbury tries to dodge the railgun (27). With her engines dead, she can't. Waiting also leaves room for the surrender call (61). A further guess, and I'm not sure of it: a miss over the planet might put the round on the forbidden surface. The film never states the reason outright.

**f. Who was lost, and when?**
- **Canterbury** (E-war destroyer), T+3:52:13: hit by railgun slugs and broke in two, "no beacons".
- **Normandy** (corvette), about T+4:25: killed by a drone that got through.

Both are confirmed "NO CARRIER" at 66. Endeavor lost its port radiator (T+5:13:16) and took a hit (T+5:13:30), but survived. The number of people killed is never given.

Enemy losses:
- Skerry's laser and mass driver (by T+1:02)
- the railgun rock (T+3:54:28)
- a frigate (T+5:13:55); its crew reached escape pods
- a second gun platform (T+5:14:40)
- Breakwater's 64 missiles (by T+7:31)
- Breakwater itself (T+7:57:29)

**g. How does it end?**
1. Breakwater is destroyed.
2. Maren asks for terms and is told to map the AI's guns, switch off Site One and shut the foundries.
3. Site One goes dark (T+8:24).
4. Endeavor, at 98% heat, dumps water, then puts out its three surviving radiators.
5. Actual calls the shields in and tells T-SEC "you're next".
6. "END OF REPORT."

My inference on why it ends there: Breakwater was the orbit's last real defender, and without her Maren can't resist. Whether the fleet then goes home: not sure.

**h. Who is the enemy, and what part does the AI play?** The enemy is the Maren Compact, a human splinter faction holding Maren, allied with "the AI" (the licensed Charon Innovations system that escaped). What the film shows of the AI is small:
- the Compact's foundries arm it;
- its weapons hide in the Breakers;
- its drones fight on their own and can't be taken over (32);
- Maren says its guns don't obey Maren (66).

The AI is never seen or heard. I'm not sure where it is, what it wants, whether it controls Breakwater or Skerry, or how this battle fits into the wider war.

## 4. Dialogue and on-screen text

**Does it sound like real fleet radio traffic? Mostly, yes.** Most lines are short and start with a callsign ("All Tidebreak, Actual.", "Breakwater, Tidebreak."). Numbers are read out digit by digit ("One-five-zero birds", "nine-four"), and lines react to what just happened. The film usually lets the picture explain the jargon. The SOURCES card ("ship logs ... laser-link traffic") also quietly explains why a cruiser's internal orders ("Lance her", "Roll to port") are heard alongside fleet calls. My sense of what real radio sounds like comes from outside the film.

**Where the on-screen text came too fast or too thick:**
- **Shots 1–6:** 68 seconds of text before the first image. That's about 17% of the running time and roughly 150 words. The reading speed is fine, at about 2.2–2.5 words a second. The trouble is how packed it is: shots 2–4 bring in about twenty new names and ideas. Shot 3 is the heaviest: Maren Compact, Maren, the AI, foundries, the Breakers, the debris ring, the warp barrier and AI-run weapons, all in 16 seconds.
- **Shot 14:** four labels appear while the clock runs and two lines play, and "BREAKWATER" and "THE BREAKERS" arrive together. This is where the names run together.
- **Shot 35:** five labels (two of them "?"), a heat readout and two lines in 11 seconds. Busy, but manageable.
- **Shot 48:** the busiest shot. It has two tracks with two times, a firing-point marker, a heat readout, and three lines introducing "spend wave", "kill wave" and "Casaba", all in 21 seconds. The heat readout competes with the plan.
- **Shot 53:** "BREAKWATER · COMPACT MONITOR · NO WARP DRIVE" is the only thing that says what Breakwater is. It gets 4 seconds in a dark, busy shot of the enemy's first appearance, which is too quick for something that important.
- **Shot 66:** the loss caption ("CANTERBURY · NORMANDY: NO CARRIER") and the surrender terms share the same 13 seconds, so the grief and the politics compete for attention.
- **What helped:** the ship-type caption on each ship's first line (for example "WALLFISH · CORVETTE") and the corner clock.

**Does the opening and closing text read as an official report?**
- **Opening:** it mostly reads as official. The header, the "RESTRICTED" marking, the numbered headings in capitals, the passive past tense, "referred to below as the AI" and "Times from warp exit." all sound right.
- **Slips in the opening:** "escaped too", "round the planet" and "shut the foundries down" sound conversational. "Artificial intelligence was outlawed after it first escaped human control" sounds like a narrator, not a staff officer. "(the Charon Innovations incident)" repeats a name already used in the same sentence.
- **Structure (my own knowledge, not from the film):** headings like Situation, Mission and Restrictions read more like pre-mission orders than an after-action report, which would usually open with a summary of the outcome and losses. Holding that back is a fair choice for drama.
- **Closing:** "END OF REPORT", in the same type as the opening, is a neat bookend. But the report never concludes in its own voice: there's no results paragraph, no statement of losses and no sign-off. It stops rather than ends, and the losses appear only as a caption on a tactical plot (66).
- **Did the opening give me what I needed? Both yes and too much at once.** Nearly every card pays off:
  - the Breakers explain where the fleet arrives;
  - the foundries come back in the surrender terms;
  - RESTRICTIONS pays off in "dazzle only";
  - T-SEC comes back in the last line;
  - "Times from warp exit" explains the clock.

  But the opening:
  - puts its most detailed sentence into Charon backstory that never comes back;
  - makes Astrid and Actual look like the same thing, through the callsign in brackets on the MISSION card;
  - never says why the surface is off-limits, which is what I most wanted to know;
  - doesn't prepare "Breakwater" or "Skerry".

## 5. Score

**7/10.** The battle itself holds together, with careful setups and payoffs (Skerry's salvo, the heat and radiators, "dazzle only", the shields), but the main target is a mystery for two-thirds of the film, the names blur together, and the AI and the political ending are too compressed to land.
