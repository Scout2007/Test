# Dialogue review, round 1: *Operation Tidebreak*, revision 7

**Read:** `storyboard/audience_script.md` (64 shots, 298 s), checked against `tidebreak_data.py` (each shot's `action`, PHASES, KEY_NUMBERS), both doctrines, `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` and cold read 7 (cited, not repeated).

**Checked:** all the proposals at once, through a copy of the builder's reading check (first tags, plot text, group windows, holds, cues). Every shot passes, and the runtime stays at 298 s. Counts include spaces and punctuation; "fits (x of y)" is the shot's or group's characters against its 12-cps ceiling.

## 1. Verdict

The film already sounds like a fleet net. Call-ups come in the right order, brevity codes are used in their proper senses, and the numbers check against the data. The faults cluster in the last two minutes, where they cost most:
- the commander gives the flagship's gunnery order;
- the hail uses an internal callsign and sets no deadline;
- the fire plan stops before the kill;
- the last line rests on an acronym the film never explains.

On top of these there is one number contradiction (three rounds, then eighteen), two captain's orders on the fleet net, and some loose terms. Every fix below is a line swap that fits in the shot's existing slack. Only the hail also needs its held silence trimmed, by 0.3 s.

## 2. Issues, ranked

**[MAJOR] 1. Shot 57, *Spinal*.**
- **Now:** "Astrid, fire." (ACTUAL)
- **Why:** Actual, the commander aboard the Astrid, authorises; the ship's fire control fires. The film has it that way at 10 ("Engage Skerry") and 24 ("Solution. Firing."). Here the commander turns gunner, which also blurs where he is (cold read).
- **Fix:** "Astrid, engage." 15 chars, 8.3 cps in the 1.8 s after its 1.2 s cue. Fits as is. The bloom and "Round away" (58) are the Astrid's answer.

**[MAJOR] 2. Shot 56, *The hail*.**
- **Now:** "Breakwater, Tidebreak. Surrender or abandon ship."
- **Why:** "Tidebreak" is the fleet's internal callsign and the operation's name. It means nothing to Breakwater, and it gives the name away on an open channel. The ultimatum also has no time limit, so the 35 s of silence before the shot reads as haste, or as a monitor deafened by the whiteout (cold read).
- **Fix:** "Breakwater, surrender or abandon ship. Thirty seconds." 54 chars.
  - It fits once the shot's `hold` drops from 0.8 to 0.5 s: 54 characters in 4.5 s is 12.0 cps, and the runtime doesn't change.
  - The clock jumps 33 s across the cut to 57, so the deadline visibly runs out before "Astrid, engage."
  - The offer to the crew, which the lore review asked for, stays.

**[MAJOR] 3. Shot 43, *The fire plan*.**
- **Now:** "First salvo decoys her point defence; second, forty Casabas, kills her drive."
- **Why:** four problems.
  - "First salvo / second" clashes with "Salvo one / two" in every later call (46, 47, 52, 54).
  - "Decoys" undersells the spend wave, which carries ~90 warheads to strip the facing side (PHASES, O3).
  - The order stops at her drive. The Astrid's task, the reason for the violet ASTRID marker, is never given, so cripple-then-kill (O10) has to be inferred (cold read, questions d–e).
  - "Casabas" is the jargon the cold reader stumbled on here. The jets at 55 define the word better than the briefing can.
- **Fix:** "Salvo one draws her point defence; salvo two cuts her drive." 60 chars. Then, on the plot's third beat (the ASTRID marker): "Astrid takes the kill." 22 chars. The shot comes to 203 of 204: fits.

**[MAJOR] 4. Shots 13 and 44, *Skerry burns* and *The net*.**
- **Now:** "Laser and driver down. Eighteen rounds inbound." / "Pellet cloud dead ahead. Skerry's rounds, on time."
- **Why:** Skerry throws three rounds every ten minutes from T+0:04 to T+0:54: six throws, 18 rounds. The film reports only the first three, so "eighteen" contradicts "three". "On time" is a nod to the viewer (cold read).
- **Fix:**
  - "Laser and driver down. Fifteen more inbound." 44 chars; fits (44 of 48).
  - "Pellet cloud dead ahead. Skerry's first rounds." 47 chars; fits (80 of 84).
  - "First" picks up "ARRIVE T+6:40" (8) and pairs with "Skerry's last rounds" (46).

**[MAJOR] 5. Shot 63, *Hold*.**
- **Now:** "Nauvoo, Actual. Bring the shields in. T-SEC, you're next."
- **Why:** The last spoken line rests on an acronym the film never explains. "You're next" was chosen over "the sky's yours" because Sites 2–4 are still live; the fix keeps that.
- **Fix:** "Nauvoo, Actual. Bring the shields in. T-SEC, you land next." 59 chars; fits (59 of 60). It names T-SEC by its job and says why the fleet fought: to take the orbit so troops can land.

**[MINOR] 6. Shots 37 and 39, *Lance channels* and *Broadside*.**
- **Now:** "Forty-five klicks. Lance her." / "Roll to port. Batteries on that gun."
- **Why:** These are the captain's orders to his helm and gunners, not fleet traffic. On the net a ship reports what it is doing.
- **Fix:**
  - "Forty-five klicks. Lancing her." 31 chars; fits (31 of 48).
  - "Rolling to port. Engaging that gun." 35 chars; fits (35 of 120). "Engaging" is the report that answers a commander's "engage".
  - "Shutters!" (16) and "Radiators in!" (34) read as reflex reports and can stay.

**[MINOR] 7. Shot 9, *The picture*.**
- **Now:** "…under Site One's cover." / "ROE: no fire on the surface. Skerry's laser burns radiators; it dies first."
- **Why:**
  - "The surface" seems to cover Skerry's surface too, which the fleet then strikes (cold read).
  - Nothing yet says Site One is a laser.
  - "Burns radiators" tells crews what they already know; an order does the same job.
- **Fix:** "…under Site One's laser." 22 chars, the same length. Then "ROE: no fire on the planet. Skerry's laser dies first. Radiators stay in." 73 chars. Fits (189 of 192).

**[MINOR] 8. Shot 14, *Fins edge-on*.**
- **Now:** ENDEAVOR: "Skerry's blind. Radiators out, edge-on to the planet."
- **Why:**
  - Running the fins out is fleet-wide (the Astrid's fins glow behind), so it's Actual's call, as "Radiators in" is at 15.
  - "Blind" is wrong: the laser was destroyed, and Skerry's rounds still come.
  - The planet is never named aloud.
- **Fix:** ACTUAL: "All Tidebreak, radiators out, edge-on to Maren." 47 chars; fits (47 of 60). It also answers 9's "Radiators stay in".

**[MINOR] 9. Shot 31, *The sweep*.**
- **Now:** "Rocks done. Moonlets next."
- **Why:** The flash on Anchor's far edge is a mine a drone tripped, but nobody says so. The mines in the terms (61) then come from nowhere (cold read).
- **Fix:** "One mine. Moonlets next." 24 chars, plus the 22-character first tag; fits (46 of 48).

**[MINOR] 10. Shot 52, *The spend wave*.**
- **Now:** "Point defence lit. Salvo two going round."
- **Why:** "Going round" puzzled the cold reader. In the action, the destroyer steers the kill wave round the mounts the spend wave has unmasked.
- **Fix:** "Point defence lit. Steering salvo two round it." 47 chars; fits (47 of 48).

**[MINOR] 11. Shots 48 and 61, *The anvil* and *Terms*.**
- **Now:** "BREAKWATER · COMPACT MONITOR · NO WARP DRIVE" / "Maren is requesting terms."
- **Why:** "COMPACT" reads as an adjective, as the cold reader took it, on the largest ship in the film (~1,800 m). The faction is never named, so "Maren" in the terms could be a planet, a government or a person.
- **Fix:**
  - "BREAKWATER · MAREN COMPACT · NO WARP DRIVE" 42 chars; fits (42 of 48), and it lightens a crowded 4 s reveal.
  - "Maren Compact requesting terms." 31 chars; fits (111 of 120).

**[MINOR] 12. Shot 25, *Payback*.**
- **Now:** "That's for Canterbury."
- **Why:** It's the one movie line on the net (cold read). It reports nothing, and the shot's matched framing of 21 already makes the payback.
- **Fix:** Delete it, and keep "Splash." at its 0.5 s cue.

**[MINOR] 13. Shot 11, *Birds away*.**
- **Now:** "Birds away, one-five-zero."
- **Why:** Spoken digit by digit, a number sounds like a course or bearing, which is how the cold reader heard it. Elsewhere counts are spoken whole ("sixty-four").
- **Fix:** "One-fifty birds away." 21 chars; fits (79 of 84).

**[MINOR] 14. Shots 24 and 34, *Answer* and *Fins in*.**
- **Now:** "Spinal on the platform." / "Launch, four hundred klicks! Radiators in!"
- **Why:** One weapon type goes by three names:
  - "Railgun" (21);
  - "platform" (24), never heard before;
  - "Launch" (34), which names no weapon. It let the cold reader merge this gun with the frigate that fires 30 s later.
- **Fix:**
  - "Spinal on that railgun." 23 chars; fits (23 of 24 before the 2 s cue).
  - "Railgun, four hundred klicks! Radiators in!" 43 chars; fits (43 of 48). It also ties the new threat to Canterbury's killer.

**[MINOR] 15. Shot 5, *Shields to the park*.**
- **Now:** "Nauvoo, shield's yours. Guns clear."
- **Why:** In weapons handling, "clear" means unloaded. The cold reader took it as "ready", then found the turrets still clamped at 39. "Unmasked" says exactly what changed: the shield no longer blocks their arcs.
- **Fix:** "Nauvoo, shield's yours. Guns unmasked." 38 chars, with the Nauvoo's reply "Copy. Holding them." 19 chars. Fits (72 of 72).

**[NIT] 16. Shot 30, *Anchor*.** "Into her lee": ships are "her", rocks aren't. **Fix:** "Anchor. Into the lee, out of Site One's sight." 46 chars, the same length.

**[NIT] 17. Shot 12, *Turn and burn*.** "All Tidebreak, execute. One g, thirty-four minutes." Under the executive method, "execute" is the trigger, so it comes last. **Fix:** "All Tidebreak, one g, thirty-four minutes. Execute." 51 chars, the same length.

**[NIT] 18. Shot 49, *Sixty-four*.** The Astrid takes on two new tasks without acknowledging them. **Fix:** ACTUAL: "Astrid, cover Extenuating; take backup guidance." 48 chars, then ASTRID: "Wilco." 6 chars. Fits (120 of 120).

## 4. What works

- **Call-ups in the right order** where they matter, addressee before sender: "Infinity, Actual. Engage Skerry.", "All Tidebreak, Actual.", "Nauvoo, Actual."
- **Execution reports serve as acknowledgements,** as on a busy tactical net: "Endeavor, burning.", "Solution. Firing.", "Flipping.", "Salvo one away."
- **Brevity codes in their proper senses,** each decoded by the picture: vampire, leaker, break, terminal, and "splash" in its air-defence sense of a kill at both 25 and 50.
- **Apart from the round count (#4), every number checks against the data and the clock:** fourteen ships (eleven burning, three dark), six and a half hours to T+6:40, one g for 34 minutes, 400 and 45 km, sixty-four cells, both TOTs (52, 55), and 2:49 to T+7:57:29.
- **No line outruns what its ship can know.** The Astrid, the fleet's eye, makes the long-range calls; the Extenuating calls what its drones light. Light-lag stays under about 1.5 s, and no exchange needs a faster reply.
- **The register is calm and lean:** "Canterbury's breaking up. No beacons."; "Sweep's not done." / "Can't wait. Radiators out.", a captain's call on his own ship that causes the next disaster; "Site One is cold."
- **The terms are militarily sound:** charts rather than an order to stand down, because the emplacements have no remote override (HD9).
