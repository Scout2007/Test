# Round 1 review: cinematography and editing

## Verdict

The four-act shape and the order of beats are right, and several shots already sell scale honestly. As timed, though, the film can't be watched and read at the same time. Text is on screen for about 80% of the running time, 31 of 37 shots last 3–5 s whatever they carry, and at the stated lenses most kills are a few pixels wide. To fix it: re-time to about 3:20, cut the subtitle load by a third or more, add long-lens coverage and a clock and HUD plan, and set up the Canterbury, the Normandy and Breakwater before they matter.

## Issues, ranked

### 1. [MAJOR] Subtitles bury the picture (film-wide; worst are 5, 28, 32 and 36)
- **Problem:** 36 of 37 shots carry comm lines, 51 in all. At 15 cps, with at least 1 s per line, they need about 132 of the film's 165 s, so the viewer spends the film reading instead of watching. The setup (5), the geography (28) and the resolution (36) can't be read at all.
- **Evidence:** Characters per second, text only:

  | Shot | cps | Note |
  |---|---|---|
  | 28 | 29 | 117 characters in 4 s, on top of the HUD labels |
  | 5 | 26 | |
  | 32 | 25 | |
  | 36 | 22 | |
  | 6, 20, 30 | 19 | |
  | 7, 26 | 16 | |
  | 4, 12, 13, 24, 31 | about 15–16 | |
  | 17 | — | three speakers and an internal cut in 5 s |

  Speaker tags add to the load: EXTENUATING CIRCUMSTANCES (25 characters) appears 6 times and TIDEBREAK ACTUAL 16 times.
- **Fix:** Don't subtitle what the picture already shows: drop 2's line and trim the lines in 11, 14, 21 and 25. Keep lines that say who, where or when, or that carry grief. Shorten the tags to ACTUAL (TIDEBREAK ACTUAL · ASTRID on first use), EXTENUATING, ASTRID and ENDEAVOR, set small and dim. No line should straddle a cut; if one must, hold it at least 12 frames past the cut. At the durations in #2, every rewrite below reads at 12 cps or less:
  - **5:** "Skerry's thrown three rounds down our lane." / "They'll be hours." Tag the HUD **ARRIVES T+6:40**. The sketch's "TOF 6 h 40 m" from T+0:04 lands at T+6:44, not at shot 30's T+6:40.
  - **6:** "Picture's up. Breakwater's right where the brief said."
  - **7:** "Infinity, wave one: take Skerry's laser off the board." / "Wave one away."
  - **9:** "Splash on Skerry. Their laser's gone." Viewers will read "Two tracks down" as two of the three incoming rounds.
  - **12:** "Site One's painting us! Shutters!"
  - **13:** "Launch, launch! Cold birds off the rocks!"
  - **20:** "One's through—on Normandy!" Move "Normandy's gone. Close the gap." to the head of 21, and shorten 21's own line to "Turnover. One at a time."
  - **26:** "Frigate off the rock, sixty k! Engaging."
  - **28:** "The Lee, Breakwater, Site One: one line. Spend wave on my mark." Cut the Excelsior line.
  - **29:** "Mark." / "Spend wave away." Cut the Infinity line.
  - **30:** "Skerry's rounds. Right on time." / "All ships, left two degrees."
  - **31:** "Breakwater's tracking. The ring's unmasking."
  - **32:** "Their net's down." / "All arrays: dazzle Breakwater."
  - **33:** "Kill wave, on target in three, two—"
  - **36:** "Actual, Maren's asking for terms." / "Site One goes dark first." The film never introduces "MDC".

### 2. [MAJOR] One tempo from start to finish starves the key beats (runtime)
- **Problem:** 31 of 37 shots run 3–5 s (mean 4.5 s), whether they establish, kill or end the film. There is no slow opening, no fast ambush and no breath after a death, and the climax and ending get no more time than the setups.
- **Evidence:**
  - Shot 35 holds the slew, the shot, a three-minute flight, the impact, the break-up and two lines in 7 s.
  - Shots 36–37, the costly victory, get 11 s between them.
  - The 2 h 10 min coast (10→11) is a plain cut.
  - The Canterbury breaks up, and on the very next frame the Astrid is ordered to turn.
- **Fix:** Suggested durations:

  | Shot | Now | Suggested | Why |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 6 s | 8 s | The ship needs about 3 s on screen after the bloom |
  | 3 | 5 s | 7 s | |
  | 5 + 6 | 7 s | 12 s | Restructured; see #4 |
  | 10 | 4 s | 7 s | Plays the coast on the clock |
  | 16 | 4 s | 5 s | Hold on the hiss |
  | 17 | 5 s | 8 s | |
  | 24 | 4 s | 6 s | |
  | 25 | 6 s | 9 s | |
  | 28 | 4 s | 7 s | |
  | 35 | 7 s | 10 s | |
  | 36 | 4 s | 7 s | |
  | 37 | 7 s | 12 s or more | |

  Cut the ambush (13–16) faster: 2–3 s shots with ECU inserts (#10). In 35, play the flight instead of cutting it out. After "Impact in three minutes", hold on the crippled Breakwater, its turrets swinging uselessly and the clock racing, then cut to the hit. That wait is the best suspense the film has. The total comes to about **3:20**, and the story needs that length. To stay under 3:00, fold 19 into 18 and 26 into 27, and drop the park business in 4.

### 3. [MAJOR] At real lenses, the far end of each exchange can't be seen (3, 15, 17, 24–27, 35)
- **Problem:** Kills happen at ranges from 40 km to 15,000 km, but they are framed on 28–50 mm lenses set up for the near ship.
- **Evidence (36 mm sensor):**
  - **27:** a 350 m frigate at 40 km is about 15 px wide at 28 mm. Its opening midsection and scattering pods can't be seen.
  - **26:** from over the Donnager at 40 mm, the frigate is about 10 px and the Endeavor a few dozen pixels.
  - **15:** the Canterbury, 600 km away at 50 mm, is about 1 px.
  - **3:** Skerry sits about 57° from Maren, but the 85 mm frame is only 24° wide.
- **Fix:** Add a long-lens "tracker" camera: 600–2,000 mm, heavy slow pans, sensor noise.
  - **27:** split it into a 2 s CU on the nose, then 1,000 mm on the frigate, which fills about a quarter of the frame as it breaks.
  - **26:** shoot at 600 mm from a camera on the Endeavor's hull: the frigate clears its rock and fires, and the hull camera shakes with the hit. Cut the Donnager's answer, or move it into 27's background.
  - **Show every target before it's hit, and match the framing.** 17's impact matches 15's rock. Give 24 a half-second glimpse of the platform's muzzle flash, and match 25's impact to it. Match 35's impact to 34's angle, so we see the slug enter the Casaba breach.
  - **3:** drop Skerry from the frame; 5 introduces it.

### 4. [MAJOR] No clock or HUD plan, so the viewer loses place and time (Acts II–IV)
- **Problem:** 32 of 36 cuts jump time, but the board never says how the clock behaves. There are only three full HUD inserts (5, 19 and 28), each 3–4 s long. None comes at the ambush or at the Lee, where three threats arrive from three places we have never seen.
- **Evidence:**
  - In 17, the Astrid is 8,000 km back, and that distance is never shown.
  - At the Lee, the platform's rock (800 km away) and the frigate's rock (60 km away) are never established.
  - The five biggest jumps are all plain cuts: 8→9 (38 min), 10→11 (2 h 10 min), 21→22 (34 min), 29→30 (58 min) and 36→37 (1 h 25 min).
  - Shot 5, the Astrid's telescope feed, plays before shot 6 shows the Astrid's dish slewing, so we see what the eye sees before we see the eye.
- **Fix:**
  - **Clock:** small and persistent, in one corner, clear of the subtitles.
    - Start it on the flash in shot 1 at T+0:00:00.
    - Run it at the shot's rate: spinning seconds say "compressed" and crawling seconds say "slowed".
    - For jumps of 30 minutes or more, roll the digits for 12–18 frames or dissolve.
  - **HUD:** one master plot, with Maren screen right as in the live action.
    - Each insert runs at least 5 s and carries no more than 4 labels.
    - The clock and "ENDEAVOR SINK nn %" sit in the same place every time, so the heat clock builds toward 23.
  - **Where to use the HUD:**
    - **6:** restructure as the dish (3 s), then 5's Skerry feed (4 s), then the master plot (5 s) showing Breakwater, Site 1, Skerry, the Breakers and the approach line.
    - **17:** the Astrid, 8,000 km back, swings its bearing line onto the platform. This shows the slew honestly.
    - **22:** the Lee's shadow, the fleet strung along it, and two nearby rocks tagged "?".
    - **28:** the line, as now.

### 5. [MAJOR] The losses and the antagonist aren't set up (6, 11, 15–20, 29–36)
- **Problem:** The Canterbury is never seen or heard before it dies, and the Normandy never speaks. Breakwater first appears at 2:11 and is dead by 2:34. The three missile waves blur together.
- **Evidence:**
  - The hiss in 16 and "Remember the Cant." in 17 land on a ship with no face.
  - Viewers will read 29's launch as 33's arrival.
  - The spend wave's arrival at T+6:01, which explains why the kill wave gets through, is never shown.
  - Nothing in 36–37 recalls the ships that were lost.
- **Fix:**
  - **The Canterbury:** give it a shot and a line early, such as its dish unfolding in 6 ("Net's up. Going quiet.") or leading the lane in 11.
  - **The Normandy:** give it 18's line.
  - **Breakwater:** show it in 6's plot as a real telescope image, a hard grey silhouette over Maren's limb, so that 31 becomes a reveal.
  - **The waves:** say "spend wave" and "kill wave" in the lines, as the phase table already does. Add a 3 s distant shot at T+6:01 of the spend wave dying as a flicker over Maren's limb (class D).
  - **The losses:** add one callback in 36–37, such as the two dead channels on a last plot.

### 6. [MAJOR] Time compression makes kilometre-long hulls look like models (17, 24, 25, 35; also 8 and 21)
- **Problem:** Big bodies moving fast read as miniatures, and the one fully built weapon plays at fast-forward.
- **Evidence:**
  - In 17, the 1.65 km Astrid finishes its ~30 s slew in about 1 s of screen time (26×).
  - In 25, the M-1C's 6–9 s wake-up plays in about 1 s (7×). The roll, the wake, the shots and the 32 s flight don't fit into 42 s of mission time in any case.
  - Shot 24 is labelled 1:1, yet it contains the 32 s slug flight.
- **Fix:** Keep rotations at 3–4× or slower, cut out the middle, and let the clock jump.
  - **25:** the roll at 5× (3 s); then the wake and the first shots at 1:1 on a hull camera (5 s: lids, cells, shell, bore pulse, the 7 m recoil, the fins); then a 1.5 s cut to the rock flash, with the clock jumping 32 s.
  - **24:** the call; then the fins crawling in against the clock at about 8×, where the race itself is the suspense; then the hit at 1:1.
  - **17 and 35:** show only the last degrees of each slew, at 1:1.
  - **8 and 21:** consider 4× instead of 8×, with a cut.

### 7. [MINOR] No screen-direction rule, and the sketches contradict the action (8, 11, 15, 16, 17, 21, 27)
- **Problem and evidence:** In the sketch library an unflipped ship's bow points left. On that basis:
  - Sketches 8 and 21 put the plume at the bow.
  - Sketch 11 puts the corvettes that are "ahead" behind the Endeavor's stern.
  - In sketch 15, the Canterbury faces away from a platform that is "off its bow".
  - In sketches 17 and 27, the muzzle bloom and the lance glow sit at the stern.
  - Shot 16's action says "holed stern to bow". That wording is left over from the draft's braking destroyer: a ship flying bow-first is holed bow to stern.
- **Fix:**
  - Keep Maren and the enemy screen right; the LREF travels and fires left to right.
  - After 21, bows point left and plumes point right while travel stays left to right, so the flip reads.
  - Allow enemy reverse angles (15, 31, 33, 34) only over an enemy foreground, with the fleet coming head-on.
  - At the Lee, stage 25–27 with the bow toward the frigate's rock. On map C the two threats are about 138° apart as seen from the Endeavor, so the platform sits off the port quarter and the frigate off the port bow. The port broadside, the port-belt hit and the nose lance then share one screen axis.

### 8. [MINOR] Camera bodies and the sound rule (4, 7, 8, 11, 13, 17, 20, 21, 22, 29, 36)
- **Problem:** Several shots use "through the hull" sound on free-flying cameras, and a "handheld" camera (7) has no operator in space.
- **Fix:** Tag every shot with one of three camera types, and sound follows:
  - **Hull camera:** rigid and shakes with the ship. The only kind that hears anything.
  - **Drone:** floats; silent.
  - **Tracker:** the long lens from #3; silent.

  Then fix these shots:
  - **7:** mount the camera on the Infinity's hull, shaking with each launch.
  - **4:** mount it on the shield. The clamps bang, then silence as it parts. The Nauvoo is behind this camera, so take it out of the action.
  - **8, 17 and 21:** use sub-bass rather than hull roar, or hull-mount 21.
  - **11, 22 and 29:** score or silence.
  - **13:** no hiss in a vacuum.
  - **20:** the Wallfish's hull can't carry the sound of the Normandy breaking up. Let the Normandy's channel die to hiss instead, echoing 16 and making it the motif for a loss.
  - **36:** land the drifting camera on the hull at the end, and only then fade the ticking in.

### 9. [MINOR] No lighting plan, and the reference frames suggest the wrong scale (3, 10, 22–25, 37)
- **Problem and evidence:**
  - The board gives no sun direction anywhere.
  - The reference frames for 10 and 37 show a daylit planet filling the frame from low orbit. In the story, Maren is about 1.8° across at T+1:10 (about 70 px at 40 mm) and about 14° from 54,000 km.
  - Map D stands the fleet off at 54,000 km, but 37 says "city lights below". Those give two different frames.
- **Fix:**
  - Adopt the sun direction the board already implies (a crescent Maren in 3, the night side in 37): the sun is behind Maren.
  - The Endeavor near the Lee then sits in the rock's real shadow, so "in the lee" reads as darkness and the fins glow against black (the fins1dark look, 23–25).
  - Site 1 falls on the night side, so in 37 its lights can visibly go out, which gives "Site One's dark" its picture.
  - Show stars only where a real exposure would catch them (1, 3 and the feeds), never behind a sunlit hull.

### 10. [MINOR] A narrow lens range and repeated set-ups (8/21, 10, 14, 37)
- **Problem and evidence:** 29 of the 35 shots with a lens sit between 24 and 50 mm. There are 4 close-ups and no extreme close-ups. Shots 8 and 21 are the same set-up: WS, 28 mm, a 90° orbit, the drive lighting.
- **Fix:**
  - Add 1–1.5 s ECUs that the rig already supports: the CIWS spinning up (14), the M-1C charge cells (25), a lens going violet (32), the CIWS spinning down (36).
  - Make 8 a long-lens shot of the whole group lighting up together. The action promises that, and a 28 mm orbit on one ship can't show it. Make 21 a flip filmed from a hull camera.
  - In 10, start off-axis while the fins extend, then arc to dead ahead so they collapse to slivers. The move itself explains "edge-on".
  - In 37, frame the Endeavor end-on, so three glowing arms and a gap read as a broken cross at any size. At 1:1 the fins take about 20 s to extend, so start them part-way out.

### 11. [MINOR] Dangling beats cost reading time (26, 28, 29, 31)
- Four beats never pay off on screen: the Donnager's answer, the Excelsior's line about the shield park, the Infinity heading home, and Breakwater's open missile cells. Cut them, or pay off the missile cells.

### 12. [NIT] Board housekeeping
- **Internal cuts:** 6, 12, 17 and 35 (and the splits proposed in #3 and #6) contain cuts inside the shot. List them as a/b shots so each cut gets its own duration, clock reading and subtitle timing. In 17 and 35 the clock should jump at the cut rather than "compress 26×/30×".
- **Ship count in 3:** the VFX list has 12 flashes, the mission timeline says "13 exits", the fleet list names 14 hulls, and the line says "Thirteen for thirteen". Make these agree with what's on screen.
- **Act break:** shots 18–20 are still the Breakers fight, so the act break belongs at 21.
- **Naming:** "the lee of the Lee" is hard to parse at reading speed. Rename the rock in the lines.

## What works
- **The skeleton:** arrival, long reach, the Lee, then hammer and anvil. It has real setups and payoffs: the spinal cannon (17 → 35), Skerry's rounds (5 → 30), and the fins as a running thread (10, 11, 23, 24, 28, 36, 37).
- **Honest scale shots:**
  - **9:** at 1,200 mm, Skerry is a disc a quarter of the frame wide at about 260,000 km, and its flashes are a second old.
  - **11:** the rock passes at true speed. At 24 mm, a rock 20 km off crosses the 30 km-wide frame in about 2 s.
  - **22:** the boulder field under the Endeavor.
  - **29:** looking along the line from the rock's surface, with Maren a disc about 5° across.
- **Sound:** most notes already follow the Expanse rule. 3's thuds as score, 33's "no air, no bangs", 35's drop-out then a single boom, and 16's channel dying to hiss are all good.
- **Time and continuity:** slow motion is saved for the kill (33, 34), against a compressed approach. The board tracks time treatment shot by shot, which makes a clock design possible, and it carries damage from shot to shot: the fins, the stump, the scars, the shield.
